 Invoice Trust Protocol (ITP): Discovery, Delivery, and Verification of
                          Electronic Invoices
                     draft-martinez-itp-core-latest

Abstract

   This document specifies the core of the Invoice Trust Protocol (ITP).
   ITP lets a business in any country find where and in which syntaxes
   another business receives invoices, deliver a signed invoice to it
   over HTTP, and obtain a signed acknowledgment.  It also lets any
   later holder of the invoice, such as the buyer, a bank, or an
   auditor, check who issued it, that it was not altered, and which tax
   or network registrations exist for it.  ITP carries invoices in
   existing syntaxes, defines no invoice data model, and works alongside
   the channels that tax authorities and networks make mandatory.
   Commercial statuses, payment information, and agent mandates are
   specified in companion documents.

1.  Introduction

   Business-to-business invoices travel as PDF files attached to email,
   through supplier portals, through four-corner networks such as Peppol
   [PEPPOL-BIS] and the Digital Business Networks Alliance [DBNA],
   through EDI value-added networks, and through clearance or reporting
   platforms run by tax authorities.  The OECD [OECD-TA3] describes
   these platforms and how tax administrations use them.  None of these
   channels gives the parties a common way to:

   *  find where, and in which syntaxes, a given legal entity receives
      invoices, unless both are in the same network;

   *  check, without trusting the channel that delivered it, that an
      invoice comes from the entity named in it and was not modified,
      including the account it gives for payment; and

   *  check which tax registration, clearance, or network acknowledgment
      exists for that invoice.

   Payment redirection through forged invoices or changed bank details
   is a common fraud.  The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center
   attributes about 2.8 billion US dollars of reported losses in 2024 to
   business email compromise [IC3-2024], a category that includes
   requests to change payment details.  Mandatory structured electronic
   invoicing is spreading in every region [OECD-TA3]: in Latin America,
   where it started, in Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Europe.  In
   the European Union, Directive (EU) 2025/516 [VIDA] makes it the rule
   for most intra-EU business-to-business supplies from 1 July 2030.
   This will increase the number of structured invoices exchanged across
   networks and borders.

   This document defines:

   *  participant identifiers that reuse existing schemes (Section 4);

   *  discovery of receiving capabilities through DNS and a well-known
      URI (Section 5);

   *  a trust model for participant keys (Section 6);

   *  a signed envelope that carries an invoice in its original syntax,
      plus evidence added after signing (Section 7);

   *  an HTTP binding for delivery, copies for the issuer, and status
      messages (Section 8);

   *  the format of status messages and the two statuses that delivery
      needs (Section 9);

   *  registration of commitments to envelopes in transparency services
      [RFC9943] (Section 10); and

   *  a verification procedure (Section 11).

   Two companion documents build on it.  [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle]
   specifies commercial and payment statuses, payment information, and
   models of jurisdictions and networks.
   [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates] lets a business authorize a
   software agent to act within limits.  This document can be
   implemented without the companion documents.

1.1.  Scope

   ITP carries invoices in syntaxes defined elsewhere: national formats
   prescribed by tax authorities, syntaxes based on [EN16931] or Peppol
   PINT [PEPPOL-PINT], such as [UBL] and [CII], [GOBL], and EDI
   standards such as ASC X12 [X12] and UN/EDIFACT [EDIFACT].  ITP
   assumes no semantic model; Appendix A is informative.  Tax treatment
   and the execution of payments are out of scope.  Where law or a
   network agreement mandates a channel, ITP runs alongside it, and the
   invoice produced or validated by that channel is the legal invoice
   (Section 7.3).

1.2.  Relationship to Existing Work

   Peppol and the Digital Business Networks Alliance [DBNA] locate a
   recipient's access point through U-NAPTR records [RFC4848] in a zone
   run by the network, as specified by OASIS BDXL [BDXL], and through a
   service metadata publisher.  The Digital Business Networks Alliance
   profiles OASIS SMP 2.0 [SMP2] and the OASIS AS4 profile [BDX-AS4];
   Peppol uses its own SMP specification [PEPPOL-SMP] and AS4 profile
   [PEPPOL-AS4].  Those networks admit access points by contract and
   certificate.  ITP reuses their identifiers; an ITP envelope can be
   carried over them, and a Participant Document can point to a Peppol
   endpoint.  Appendix D.8 explains why ITP does not reuse BDXL for
   discovery.

   EDI value-added networks and exchange platforms that tax authorities
   run or designate address participants by their own identifiers.  ITP
   treats them as it treats Peppol: their identifiers can be registered
   as PID schemes (Section 4), their endpoints as channel types
   (Section 5.5), and an ITP envelope can be carried over any of them
   that carries arbitrary documents.

   The IETF EDIINT working group defined AS1 [RFC3335], AS2 [RFC4130],
   and AS3 [RFC4823] for signed EDI exchange with signed receipts.
   Their S/MIME signatures cover the transferred payload, but keys are
   exchanged when the trading partnership is established and
   certificates may be self-signed (Section 8 of [RFC4130]), so a party
   outside that partnership cannot tie a signature to a legal entity.
   ITP signs the invoice itself, independently of any transfer, and
   binds the key to the legal entity through attestations, so that third
   parties that later hold it, such as banks, auditors, or factors, can
   check it.  An earlier individual draft
   [I-D.jaakola-simple-invoicing-protocol] addressed parties by email
   address and was not pursued.  Registered delivery services
   [ETSI-ERDS], including registered electronic mail [ETSI-REM], give
   evidence of sending and receipt and can complement ITP's signed
   acknowledgments.

   UN/CEFACT and OASIS define the Exchange Header Envelope [XHE], the
   successor of the UN/CEFACT Standard Business Document Header, which
   references or contains business documents without changing them and
   can be signed.  ITP defines a JSON envelope so that it can be signed
   with JOSE, registered in SCITT transparency services, and carry
   evidence added after signing (Section 7).  An XHE envelope can be
   carried as the document of an ITP envelope.

   Two current efforts address parts of the same problem.  The FIDES
   Direct e-invoicing track [FIDES-DEI] tests business wallets,
   decentralized identifiers, verifiable credentials, and qualified
   seals for identifying the parties to an invoice and for federated
   discovery alongside Peppol.  UN/CEFACT is specifying verifiable trade
   documents, including the commercial invoice [UNVTD], and the UN
   Transparency Protocol [UNTP], in which an authoritative register,
   such as a business register, issues a credential that binds a
   registered entity to its identifier and optionally to a decentralized
   identifier.  ITP does not require wallets or decentralized
   identifiers: it uses DNS, HTTPS, JOSE, and SCITT, and adds delivery,
   status messages, and transparency.  An attestation issuer can rely on
   credentials from business wallets when it issues ITP attestations
   (Section 6.3), and documents defined by UNVTD can be carried in an
   ITP envelope.

   Organizational identity can come from several sources: business and
   tax registers, certificates issued under electronic signature laws
   (for example qualified certificates under [EIDAS2] in the European
   Union, and certificates issued under the laws of other jurisdictions,
   for which the UNCITRAL Model Laws on Electronic Signatures
   [UNCITRAL-MLES] and on identity management and trust services
   [UNCITRAL-MLIT] give a basis for recognition across borders), the
   verifiable LEI [VLEI] [ISO17442-3], verifiable credentials [W3C-VC],
   or business wallets such as the European Business Wallet proposal
   [EU-BW].  Certificates are used directly under rule 3 of Section 6.2,
   and attestation issuers can rely on the other sources when they issue
   ITP attestations (Section 6.3).  ITP does not define a new identity
   scheme.

1.3.  Why the IETF

   This document specifies a DNS node name, a well-known URI, an HTTP
   binding, JOSE and COSE profiles, and a use of SCITT transparency
   services.  No single IETF working group covers all of these.  The
   closest are SCITT (transparency), KEYTRANS (key transparency),
   HTTPAPI (idempotency), WEBBOTAUTH (HTTP signatures for automated
   clients), and, for the credentials used by ITP and by
   [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates], OAUTH (SD-JWT VC and status
   lists).  The author asks DISPATCH whether this document fits an
   existing group or needs a BoF.  The meaning of invoice fields and
   lifecycle codes stays with CEN, OASIS, UN/CEFACT, OpenPeppol and
   other network operators, and tax authorities.

1.4.  The Experiment

   This document is Experimental.  The experiment is whether:

   1.  at least two independent implementations interoperate on
       discovery, delivery, signature verification, and transparency
       receipts, using the test vectors in Appendix C;

   2.  at least two recipients verify envelopes that reach them over
       channels other than ITP, for example as email attachments, over
       Peppol or an EDI value-added network, or from a tax authority
       platform, and publish the share of envelopes that failed
       verification and why; and

   3.  at least one operator of an existing network or regulated
       platform publishes ITP discovery data for its participants.

   Results will be reported on the mailing list where this document is
   discussed and in later revisions of it.  The experiment ends 24
   months after publication, when the author expects either to propose a
   Standards Track revision or to declare the experiment concluded.

2.  Conventions and Definitions

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

2.1.  JSON

   JSON texts [RFC8259] defined by this document MUST be I-JSON
   [RFC7493].  Receivers MUST reject ITP JSON texts that contain
   duplicate member names, unpaired surrogates, or nesting deeper than
   32 levels, before any canonicalization or signature check.  Signed
   objects are serialized with the JSON Canonicalization Scheme (JCS)
   [RFC8785] before signing (Appendix D.1).  To keep JCS output stable
   across implementations, ITP objects MUST NOT contain non-integer
   numbers or integers whose absolute value exceeds 2^53 - 1, and
   receivers reject objects that do.

2.2.  Amounts

   Monetary amounts are strings that match the "amount" rule below,
   paired with a currency code [ISO4217]:

   amount = ["-"] ( "0" / %x31-39 *DIGIT ) ["." 1*DIGIT]

   An amount has at most 18 digits before the decimal point and no more
   decimals than the minor unit of its currency in [ISO4217], which is 0
   for some currencies and 3 for others.  A receiver rejects an envelope
   whose amounts break these rules with "envelopeInvalid".  The value
   "-0", and any value equal to zero with a minus sign, is invalid.
   Amounts are compared as numbers, not as strings.  The amount syntax
   is a wire format, independent of the decimal separator used in
   documents and displays.  A verifier does not reject an envelope
   because its currency code was withdrawn after the issue date; the
   minor unit is then that of the code at the issue date.

2.3.  Text and Language

   Strings that identify documents, such as "invoice_number", are UTF-8
   strings in Unicode Normalization Form C [UAX15] that conform to the
   FreeformClass of [RFC8264].  The issuer writes them in the core after
   removing leading and trailing spaces, after applying the width
   mapping described in Section 5.2.1 of [RFC8264], which maps fullwidth
   and halfwidth characters (common in Chinese, Japanese, and Korean
   input) to their decomposition mappings, and after any further
   mapping, such as case mapping, that a profile in "profiles" defines
   for the numbering scheme.  Digits SHOULD be ASCII digits.  Where the
   tax authority records a number with other decimal digits, such as
   Arabic-Indic digits, the core carries it as recorded, and a receiver
   that checks for duplicates first maps characters of General Category
   Nd to the ASCII digits with the same value.  Bidirectional formatting
   characters, such as U+200F RIGHT-TO-LEFT MARK, are not allowed by the
   FreeformClass and are removed from a value taken from the document.
   Receivers compare these strings code point by code point, and check
   consistency with the document after applying the same mappings to the
   value in the document.  Other strings are compared after
   Normalization Form C; general compatibility normalization (NFKC) is
   not applied, because it changes meaningful characters in some
   scripts.

   Members that hold human-readable text, such as names and reason
   texts, are UTF-8 strings.  An object that contains such members MAY
   contain "lang", a language tag [RFC5646] that applies to all of them;
   Section 16 gives details.

2.4.  Other Conventions

   Timestamps are "date-time" values of [RFC3339] in UTC.  Calendar
   dates, such as the issue date of an invoice, are "full-date" values
   in the time zone determined as described for "issue_tz"
   (Section 7.2).  Identifiers of ITP objects are UUID version 7 values
   [RFC9562].  Base64url encoding follows Section 2 of [RFC7515] without
   padding.  SHA-256 is specified in [RFC6234] and HMAC in [RFC2104].
   URIs follow [RFC3986].  ABNF follows [RFC5234] and [RFC7405].
   Examples fold long lines as described in [RFC8792].

   All identifiers of legal entities in examples are fictitious and have
   no valid check digits.  Domain names are taken from [RFC2606].
   Values in examples that are not in the initial contents of a registry
   are illustrative.

2.5.  Terminology

   The terms Signed Statement, Transparency Service, Receipt,
   Transparent Statement, Registration Policy, Auditor, and Relying
   Party are used as defined in [RFC9943].  "Origin" and "same-origin"
   are used as in [RFC6454].  In addition:

   Participant:  A legal entity, or a natural person acting in business,
      that issues or receives invoices.

   Seller, buyer:  The parties to the supply that an invoice documents.

   Issuer:  The party that issues a given document and signs its
      envelope.  It is the seller, except in self-billing (the buyer)
      and in issuance by a third party on the seller's behalf.  Where a
      tax authority platform, an authorized provider, or a fiscal device
      generates or numbers the invoice, the issuer is still the party in
      whose name it is issued, and the platform's or device's role is
      recorded as evidence.

   Node:  An HTTP server that sends and receives ITP messages for one or
      more participants.

   Operator:  The organization that runs a node.

   Directory:  A service that answers queries for Participant Documents.
      Directory answers are used only if they are accepted under
      Section 6.

   Envelope:  The signed ITP object that carries one commercial
      document, plus unsigned evidence about it (Section 7).

   Envelope core:  The JSON object that the issuer signs.

   Document:  The invoice or other commercial document in its original
      syntax.

   Legal original:  The representation of the invoice that the
      applicable law treats as the invoice (Section 7.3).

   Evidence:  Signed objects added after the issuer signature:
      renditions, stamps, and Receipts.

   Stamp:  Evidence produced by a tax authority, a network, a fiscal
      device, or a timestamping service about an envelope.

   Bridge:  A component that exchanges documents with a non-ITP channel,
      such as a tax platform, on behalf of a node.

   Attestation:  A credential in which an attestation issuer asserts an
      attribute of a participant (Section 6.3).

   Trust list:  A signed list of parties that a relying party accepts
      for given roles (Section 6.1).

   Profile:  A specification, maintained outside this document, of how
      ITP is used with a given jurisdiction or network.

   Participant Document:  A signed document in which a participant
      publishes its identifiers, keys, and receiving and sending nodes
      (Section 5.5).

   Node Document:  A signed document in which a node publishes its
      endpoints and keys (Section 5.6).

3.  Overview

3.1.  Deployment Model

   A participant normally uses a node run by its invoicing or accounting
   software provider.  The participant publishes, or lets its operator
   publish, a Participant Document that names its receiving endpoints
   and its keys.  An attestation issuer binds the participant's tax
   identifier to those keys.

   When the participant issues an invoice, its node signs an envelope,
   obtains any stamp that the issuer's tax regime requires through a
   bridge, registers a commitment to the envelope in a transparency
   service, and delivers the envelope to the recipient's node.  The
   recipient's node checks the envelope and answers with a signed
   "delivered" status.  Later statuses are specified in
   [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle].

   A recipient whose suppliers do not use ITP can publish its own
   receiving endpoint and can verify envelopes that reach it through
   other channels.

3.2.  Message Flow

    Issuer node                    Recipient's DNS, web, directory, node
        |                                               |
        |  1. DNS _itp TXT, /.well-known/itp            |
        |---------------------------------------------->|
        |  2. Participant Document, Node Document       |
        |<----------------------------------------------|
        |  3. sign envelope; stamp via bridge;          |
        |     register Signed Statement --> TS          |
        |  4. GET nonce                                 |
        |---------------------------------------------->|
        |  5. Replay-Nonce                              |
        |<----------------------------------------------|
        |  6. POST envelope (HTTP Message Signature)    |
        |---------------------------------------------->|
        |  7. 201 + "delivered" status                  |
        |<----------------------------------------------|

           Figure 1: Main message flow (TS: transparency service)

3.3.  Conformance Classes

   Sending node:  Implements discovery (Section 5), acceptance of
      Participant Documents (Section 6), envelope creation (Section 7),
      and the HTTP binding as a client (Section 8), including copies to
      the issuer's archive node (Section 8.5).  Because status messages
      are sent to "issuer_node", it also publishes a Node Document and
      accepts status messages as a server (Section 8.8).

   Receiving node:  Publishes Participant and Node Documents, implements
      the HTTP binding as a server, steps 1 to 4 of Section 11,
      duplicate detection (Section 8.4), the "copies" endpoint when a
      participant names it in "archive" (Section 8.5), and the
      "delivered" and "technical-rejected" statuses (Section 9).  A
      receiving node carries documents as octets and does not need to
      interpret them, so it MUST NOT reject a document only because it
      cannot interpret its syntax, and it answers "unsupportedDocument"
      only when the document matches no entry of the recipient's
      "accepts" (Section 5.5).  So that any two participants share at
      least one syntax, a receiving node MUST accept invoices and credit
      notes in UBL 2.1 [UBL21] or a later version, that is, documents
      with "media_type" "application/xml" whose root element is in the
      namespace "urn:oasis:names:specification:ubl:schema:xsd:Invoice-2"
      or "urn:oasis:names:specification:ubl:schema:xsd:CreditNote-2",
      whatever their customization, and deliver them to the recipient,
      even when the recipient's "accepts" does not list them; it MUST
      NOT answer "unsupportedDocument" for them.  It SHOULD also accept
      the syntax that the registered profile of each jurisdiction in
      which its participant holds a "tax" PID designates as the legal
      original, such as CFDI in Mexico.  UBL is the common base of
      Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 [PEPPOL-BIS], Peppol PINT [PEPPOL-PINT],
      the specifications of the Digital Business Networks Alliance in
      the United States [DBNA], and several national formats.  Other
      syntaxes are optional.

   Verifier:  Implements all of Section 11.  A verifier need not
      implement the HTTP binding.

   Directory:  Answers exact-match queries for Participant Documents
      (Section 5.7) and does not alter the documents it returns.

4.  Participant Identifiers

   A participant identifier (PID) has the following syntax:

   pid      = scheme ":" value
   scheme   = %x61-7A *( %x61-7A / DIGIT / "-" )
   value    = 1*( ALPHA / DIGIT / "-" / "." / ":" / "_" / pct-encoded )
   pct-encoded = "%" UPHEX UPHEX
   UPHEX       = DIGIT / %x41-46
   tax-value    = jurisdiction ":" tax-type *( ":" qualifier )
                  ":" tax-id
   jurisdiction = 2UPALPHA [ "-" 1*3( UPALPHA / DIGIT ) ]
   tax-type     = %x61-7A *( %x61-7A / DIGIT / "-" )
   qualifier    = 1*( ALPHA / DIGIT / "-" / "." / "_" )
   tax-id       = 1*( UPALPHA / DIGIT / "-" / "." / "_" / pct-encoded )
   UPALPHA      = %x41-5A

   ; The value of a "tax" PID matches tax-value.  The identifier is
   ; the last colon-separated component; a ":" in it is percent-encoded.

   Characters outside this set, such as "&" or non-ASCII letters that
   some tax identifiers contain, are percent-encoded as UTF-8 octets, as
   in Section 2.1 of [RFC3986], with uppercase hexadecimal digits.  Each
   scheme defines a normalization that MUST be applied before two PIDs
   are compared.  After normalization, two PIDs are equal only if they
   are identical octet strings.  PIDs of different schemes, or of
   different types of the "tax" scheme, are never equal, even when they
   contain the same identifier; a relation between them is established
   only by attestations (Section 6.3).  Schemes are registered in the
   ITP PID Schemes registry (Section 17.7).  The initial schemes are:

   tax:  A tax identifier of any jurisdiction, as "tax:" followed by a
      jurisdiction code in uppercase, a colon, a type from the ITP Tax
      Identifier Types registry, a colon, and the identifier.  The
      jurisdiction code is an [ISO3166] alpha-2 country code or, for
      identifiers issued by a subdivision, its [ISO3166-2] code, for
      example "CA-QC"; "XK", a user-assigned code under [ISO3166], is
      used for Kosovo.  The jurisdiction is the one whose register the
      identifier belongs to, as recorded in the registry entry, written
      with its [ISO3166] code even where documents use another prefix
      (Appendix B).  A type MAY define further colon-separated
      qualifiers before the identifier, such as a municipality code.
      Unless the registry entry for the type defines another
      normalization, the identifier is written without separators or
      spaces, in Unicode Normalization Form C, with letters in uppercase
      (letters outside ASCII by their Unicode simple uppercase mapping)
      before percent-encoding, fullwidth characters mapped to their
      ASCII equivalents, any check digit included, and without the
      country prefix that documents add to some identifiers, such as the
      prefix of VAT identification numbers in the European Union.  An
      identifier that the authority derives from a base identifier for
      an establishment or a subdivision, such as a GSTIN per Indian
      state or an Indonesian NITKU per branch, is a separate type whose
      registry entry says how it relates to the base identifier.  The
      registry entry lists the prefixes and suffixes that documents and
      validation services use for the type (Appendix B), so that two
      spellings of one identifier normalize to the same PID; for example
      "ESB00000000" in a document maps to "tax:ES:vat:B00000000".
      Examples are "tax:JP:qualified-invoice:T0000000000000",
      "tax:ES:vat:B00000000", "tax:GB:vat:000000000", and
      "tax:MX:rfc:AAA010101AA0".  Appendix B lists the initial types.  A
      party can hold several tax identifiers, for example one per state
      or one for income tax and one for consumption tax; each is a
      separate PID.  A party registered for tax in a jurisdiction other
      than its own holds a PID of that jurisdiction, for example
      "tax:FR:vat:..." for a Spanish company registered for VAT in
      France, and "seller" and "buyer" carry the PID that the document
      uses for the transaction.  Some tax identifiers are shared by
      several legal entities, such as the number of a group registration
      for VAT or another consumption tax; the registry entry for such a
      type says so.  A participant MUST NOT use a shared tax identifier
      as its primary PID; it lists it in "aliases", covered by an
      attestation that names the member by another PID.  A relying party
      MUST NOT accept a Participant Document under Section 6.2 on the
      basis of a shared tax identifier alone.

   eas:  A Peppol Electronic Address Scheme code [PEPPOL-EAS], a colon,
      and the identifier, for example "eas:0009:00000000000000" (French
      SIRET) or "eas:0088:0000000000000" (Global Location Number).
      Values are normalized to lowercase, except the hexadecimal digits
      of percent-encoded octets, since Peppol participant identifiers
      are case-insensitive [PEPPOL-IDS].

      "eas" is an initial scheme because its code list is public.  The
      list includes international schemes, such as 0088 (Global Location
      Number) and 0060 (D-U-N-S Number), and national ones, such as 0151
      (Australian Business Number).  "eas" has no precedence over other
      schemes.  Other networks, exchange platforms that tax authorities
      run or designate, and EDI value-added networks that address
      participants by identifiers of their own register PID schemes on
      the same terms.

   lei:  A Legal Entity Identifier [ISO17442] in uppercase.

   domain:  A DNS name controlled by the participant, as an A-label in
      lowercase without a trailing dot.  A "domain" PID does not
      identify a legal entity on its own (Section 6).

   Where a PID appears in a claim that requires a URI, such as the "iss"
   or "sub" claim of a JWT, it is written as the URN
   "urn:ietf:params:itp:pid:" followed by the PID, for example
   "urn:ietf:params:itp:pid:tax:MX:rfc:AAA010101AA0".

   A participant MAY hold several PIDs.  A PID is "verified" for a
   relying party only if an attestation that the relying party accepts
   binds it to the participant's keys, or if the relying party accepted
   the Participant Document under rule 3 of Section 6.2 and the
   certificate identifies the participant by that PID.

5.  Discovery

5.1.  Starting Point

   ITP discovery starts from a DNS name that the sender already knows,
   for example from a "domain" PID, from a previous invoice, or from the
   recipient's email address when the recipient controls that domain.
   Addresses at shared mailbox providers do not identify a participant.
   Finding a domain from another kind of PID is outside the scope of
   this document.  Directories that offer such lookups are caches whose
   answers are accepted only under Section 6 (Section 5.7).  In many
   economies most small businesses have no domain name of their own and
   are known to their counterparties by a tax identifier, a business
   registration number, or a telephone number.  For them, a directory
   run by a network, an operator, or a tax authority is the usual
   starting point, and a profile can designate one.  The Participant
   Document it returns is accepted under rule 1 or rule 3 of
   Section 6.2, which do not require a domain.

5.2.  Channels Required by Law or by Network Agreements

   Some jurisdictions designate the channel or registry through which
   invoices are delivered to a recipient, and some require clearance by
   the tax authority before delivery.  This document does not change
   those obligations.  ITP discovery can be used in addition, for
   example to obtain the recipient's keys or to send a copy.  An
   envelope sent through ITP in addition to a designated channel is
   marked as a copy (Section 7.2); a copy is not a separate payable.

5.3.  DNS Record

   A participant that controls a domain announces ITP support with a TXT
   record at the underscored name "_itp" [RFC8552] of that domain.  The
   record signals presence and a version, as the "_mta-sts" record of
   [RFC8461] does; all other data is retrieved over HTTPS.

   itp-record  = %s"v=ITP1" 1*( *WSP ";" *WSP itp-field ) *WSP [";"]
   itp-field   = field-name "=" field-value
   field-name  = 1*( ALPHA / DIGIT / "_" )
   field-value = 1*( %x21-3A / %x3C-7E )

   _itp.acme.example. 3600 IN TXT (
     "v=ITP1; id=20261028T0900; pid=tax:ES:vat:B00000000" )

   v:  The version.  It MUST be the first field and MUST be "ITP1".

   id:  Required.  An opaque value of 1 to 32 alphanumeric characters
      that changes whenever the participant's metadata changes.

   pid:  Optional.  The participant's primary PID.  This is a claim,
      verified only as described in Section 6.  Participants that are
      natural persons SHOULD omit it (Section 15).

   kt:  Optional.  A comma-separated list of base64url-encoded SHA-256
      JWK Thumbprints [RFC7638] of keys that may sign Participant
      Documents published for the domain.  When the record is validated
      with DNSSEC, this binds those keys to the domain (Section 6.2).
      Several thumbprints can be listed, for example for several
      participants under one domain or during a key change.

   issuers:  Optional.  A comma-separated list of attestation issuer
      identifiers, as they appear in trust lists, that the participant
      authorizes to issue attestations for its PIDs.  When the field is
      present, an attestation issuer MUST NOT issue an attestation that
      binds a key or a domain to a PID of the participant unless its own
      identifier is listed.  This is analogous to the CAA record
      [RFC8659].

   The strings of one TXT record are concatenated.  TXT records at the
   name that do not match "itp-record" are ignored.  If more than one
   TXT record at the name starts with "v=ITP1", the client MUST treat
   discovery as failed for that domain.  Unknown fields MUST be ignored.

   The record is optional; a client can use the well-known URI directly.
   The "id" field signals a change of metadata, the presence of the
   record distinguishes a withdrawn participant from a temporary HTTP
   failure, and, with DNSSEC [RFC4033], the "kt" field binds keys to the
   domain.  DNSSEC is RECOMMENDED (Section 14.3).

5.4.  Well-Known URI

   The URI "https://{domain}/.well-known/itp" returns a JSON object of
   media type "application/itp-metadata+json" that links to the
   documents published for that domain:

   {
     "id": "20261028T0900",
     "participants": [
       {"pid": "tax:ES:vat:B00000000",
        "document": "https://node.example.com/p/7f3a9c2e41d8b605"}
     ]
   }

   id (required, string):  The same value as the "id" field of the DNS
      record, if one exists.  A client that finds different values
      treats the resource as not yet updated and retries later.

   participants (required, array):  Objects with "pid" and "document",
      the URL of a Participant Document.

   Participant Document URLs SHOULD contain at least 64 bits of
   randomness, and nodes MUST NOT offer an index of the documents they
   host, so that an operator's participants cannot be listed by
   following URLs.

   Clients MUST NOT follow redirects to other origins when retrieving
   this resource.  HTTP caching follows [RFC9111].  The resource is not
   signed; trust decisions use only the signed Participant Document.

5.5.  Participant Document

   A Participant Document is a JWS [RFC7515] in the Flattened JSON
   Serialization, or in the General JSON Serialization during a key
   change (Section 6.4), with "typ" "itp-participant+jws".  Its payload
   is:

   {
     "pid": "tax:ES:vat:B00000000",
     "aliases": ["eas:9920:esb00000000", "domain:acme.example"],
     "name": "Example Logistics S.L.",
     "lang": "es",
     "seq": 7,
     "inbound": [
       {"channel": "itp",
        "node": "https://node.example.com/itp",
        "kids": ["FtIu-VbGrfe_KB6CH7GNwODB72MNxj_ml11dEvO-7kk"],
        "accepts": [
          {"media_type": "application/xml",
           "customization": "urn:cen.eu:en16931:2017"}]},
       {"channel": "peppol",
        "id": "iso6523-actorid-upis::9920:esb00000000"}
     ],
     "senders": [
       {"node": "https://node.example.com/itp",
        "kids": ["FtIu-VbGrfe_KB6CH7GNwODB72MNxj_ml11dEvO-7kk"]}],
     "jwks": {"keys": ["<participant key>", "<operator-held key>"]},
     "delegates": [
       {"kid": "<thumbprint of the operator-held key>",
        "node": "https://node.example.com/itp"}],
     "attestations": [],
     "pin_max_age": 604800,
     "predecessors": [],
     "superseded_by": null,
     "updated": "2026-10-28T09:00:00Z"
   }

   pid (required, string):  The participant's primary PID.

   aliases (optional, array of strings):  Other PIDs of the participant,
      including other tax identifiers.

   name (optional, string or array):  The registered name, or the
      registered names in several languages and scripts as described in
      Section 16, for example in Arabic and in English.  It SHOULD be
      omitted when the participant is a natural person (Section 15).

   seq (required, integer):  Increases with every new version of the
      document.

   inbound (required, array):  Receiving channels.  For "itp", "node" is
      the URL of a Node Document, "kids" lists the thumbprints of the
      node keys the participant accepts for that node, and "accepts"
      lists accepted syntaxes as a media type plus, where applicable,
      the customization identifier of the syntax (for UBL, the content
      of the CustomizationID element; for ASC X12 and UN/EDIFACT,
      carried as "application/EDI-X12" and "application/EDIFACT"
      [RFC1767], the transaction set or message type, its version, and
      any implementation guide, for example "810:005010").  A document
      matches an "accepts" entry if its "media_type" is equal to that of
      the entry and, when the entry has "customization", the document's
      "customization" is equal to it or starts with it followed by "#".
      For "peppol", "id" is the Peppol participant identifier including
      its scheme.  Channel types are registered (Section 17.7), and each
      registration states the members that identify the participant in
      that channel.  Other four-corner networks, exchange platforms that
      tax authorities run or designate, and EDI value-added networks
      register channel types on the same terms as "peppol".  Until a
      type is registered for a channel, an entry MAY use the type
      "network", with "profile", an identifier from the ITP Profiles
      registry that describes the channel, and "id", the participant's
      identifier in it.  An entry MAY contain "address", an object with
      "buyer_endpoint" (a PID, matched against "buyer" and
      "party_ids.buyer") or "buyer_reference" (a string, matched against
      "references.buyer_reference") or both; the entry applies only to
      documents in which every member present matches.  A legal entity
      can use this to route invoices for different establishments or
      departments to different endpoints.  Entries are evaluated in
      order, and a sender uses the first entry that matches the document
      and whose channel type it supports.

   senders (required, array of objects):  The nodes authorized to send
      envelopes and status messages on behalf of the participant, each
      with "node" (a Node Document URL) and "kids" (thumbprints of the
      node keys accepted for it).  A node key not listed in "kids" is
      not accepted for that node, so control of the node's web origin
      alone does not let an attacker replace its keys.

   archive (optional, object):  The node that keeps the participant's
      own copy of every envelope issued in its name, with "node" (a Node
      Document URL) and "kids" (thumbprints of the node keys accepted
      for it), as in "senders".  Nodes listed in "senders" send copies
      to it as described in Section 8.5.  A participant that issues
      through several nodes, for example from several software products,
      can name the node of its "itp" entry in "inbound", so that one
      node holds both the documents it issues and those it receives.

   jwks (required, object):  The JWK Set [RFC7517] with the
      participant's signing keys (Section 6.4).  Keys are carried by
      value so that they are covered by the signature on the document.

   delegates (optional, array):  Keys in "jwks" that an operator holds
      rather than the participant, each with "kid" and "node", the Node
      Document URL of that operator.  Only these keys produce
      "delegated-issuer" signatures.

   attestations (optional, array):  Attestations about the participant
      (Section 6.3).  Each entry is the attestation as a string, or an
      object with "attestation", the string, and "receipts", an array of
      objects with the structure of "itp_evidence.receipts" (Section 10)
      for its registration.

   pin_max_age (optional, integer):  Seconds, at most 31536000, during
      which a client that has accepted this document keeps it and treats
      payment details received through other channels as unverified
      (Section 14.3).  The value 0 removes an existing pin.

   predecessors (optional, array of objects):  Former PIDs of the
      participant after a merger, a conversion, or a change of tax
      identifier, each with "pid", "until" (a "full-date"), and
      "attestation", a "kyb" attestation whose "pids" claim contains
      both the former and the current PID.

   superseded_by (optional, string or null):  Set in the last document
      published by a former operator to the URL of the participant's new
      document.  This is a hint; the new document is accepted only under
      Section 6.2.

   updated (required, string):  Time of the last change.

   A Participant Document MUST be signed with a key in "jwks" that is
   not listed in "delegates".  Elsewhere in this document, "listed in
   senders" means listed as "node" in an entry of "senders".  An
   operator that holds only delegated keys cannot change the
   participant's identity, keys, senders, archive node, or pin.

5.6.  Node Document

   A Node Document is a JWS in the Flattened JSON Serialization with
   "typ" "itp-node+jws", signed with a key in its own "jwks".  As
   recommended by [RFC9205] and [RFC8820], this specification does not
   fix endpoint paths; they are published here:

   {
     "node": "https://node.example.com/itp",
     "versions": ["1"],
     "endpoints": {
       "nonce": "https://node.example.com/itp/nonce",
       "inbox": "https://node.example.com/itp/in",
       "envelope": "https://node.example.com/itp/env/{id}",
       "status": "https://node.example.com/itp/env/{id}/status"
     },
     "jwks": {"keys": [
       {"kty": "OKP", "crv": "Ed25519", "alg": "Ed25519",
        "kid": "FtIu-VbGrfe_KB6CH7GNwODB72MNxj_ml11dEvO-7kk",
        "x": "PUAXw-hDiVqStwqnTRt-vJyYLM8uxJaMwM1V8Sr0Zgw"}]},
     "max_envelope_size": 10485760,
     "storage": {"jurisdictions": ["ES"]},
     "meta": {"terms_of_service": "https://node.example.com/tos"}
   }

   "node" is the URL of the Node Document itself.  "versions" lists the
   major versions of the envelope core that the node accepts.  Endpoint
   values are URI Templates [RFC6570] of level 1; {id} is an envelope
   id.  The "inbox" endpoint is the same for all participants of the
   node: the recipient is taken from the signed envelope core, so that
   identifiers do not appear in URLs or in access logs.  A node named in
   "archive" of a Participant Document also publishes a "copies"
   endpoint (Section 8.5).  A signed GET request to "envelope" returns
   the envelope as received and all status messages the node holds for
   it.  The node answers such a request, and a GET request to a location
   returned with status 202, only if it is signed by a node listed in
   "senders" of the accepted Participant Document of the issuer or of
   the recipient of that envelope; otherwise it answers 404, as for an
   envelope it does not hold.  "jwks" holds the keys that the node uses
   for HTTP Message Signatures, operator seals, and the statuses it
   signs as a node.  A Node Document is trusted only because a
   participant lists it in "senders", "inbound", or "archive", or
   because an "operator" entry of an accepted trust list has the Node
   Document URL as its "id", and only its keys listed in that entry's
   "kids" (or in the trust list entry's "jwks") are accepted.
   "max_envelope_size" is in octets.  "storage" describes where the node
   stores envelopes: "jurisdictions" lists [ISO3166] alpha-2 codes, and
   "authority_access" (optional, array of strings) names tax authorities
   that have online access to them.  "meta" holds informational links
   and is not processed.

5.7.  Directories

   A directory returns Participant Documents unchanged.  It answers
   exact-match queries on a PID only; it MUST NOT offer search by name
   or by partial identifier, and it applies rate limits per client
   (Section 15).  A directory is identified by a URI Template [RFC6570]
   of level 1 with the variable "pid", configured locally or named by a
   profile; a GET request to its expansion with the PID in URN form
   returns the Participant Document with media type "application/itp-
   participant+jws", or status 404.  A directory MAY answer only
   requests signed as in Section 8.2 by nodes of operators on a trust
   list it accepts, and rate-limit per operator, so that bulk queries
   are attributable.  Answers from a directory carry no more trust than
   the signatures and attestations in the documents returned.

6.  Trust and Keys

   DNS, web servers, and directories only distribute ITP documents.  A
   document is bound to a legal entity by attestations from issuers that
   the relying party accepts.

6.1.  Trust Lists

   A trust list is a JWS with "typ" "itp-trust-list+jws".  Its payload
   has "issuer", "seq", "updated", "exp" (after which the whole list is
   no longer used), and "entries", an array of objects with: "id" (an
   HTTPS URL that identifies the entry), "role" (from the ITP Trust List
   Roles registry, Section 17.7), "jwks", "types" (attestation types it
   may issue, for attestation issuers), "schemes" (values of the ITP
   Bridge Schemes registry, for bridges), "jurisdictions" ([ISO3166]
   codes), "nbf", and "exp".  The initial roles are "attestation-
   issuer", "operator", "transparency-service", "witness", and "bridge".

   "jurisdictions" contains [ISO3166] alpha-2 codes or [ISO3166-2]
   subdivision codes.  A relying party MUST NOT accept an attestation
   that binds a "tax" PID, in "sub" or in a claim such as "pids", or a
   PID of another scheme whose registry entry ties the identifier to one
   jurisdiction (for "eas", an EAS code that designates a national
   identifier), unless that jurisdiction, or the country of that
   subdivision code, is in the "jurisdictions" of the issuer's entry.
   An entry that lists a subdivision covers only PIDs with that
   subdivision code.  An entry without "jurisdictions" covers no such
   PID.  "issuer", "seq", "exp", and "entries" are required, as are
   "id", "role", and "jwks" in each entry, and a trust list replaces an
   earlier one from the same issuer only if its "seq" is greater.

   How relying parties obtain trust lists, and which they accept, is a
   matter of local configuration, as for other trust anchors.  Trust
   lists can be derived from existing registers, such as the trusted
   lists of qualified trust service providers [ETSI-TL], registers of
   Peppol service providers, or national registers of accredited
   platforms.  Section 14.2 discusses the consequences.

6.2.  Accepting a Participant Document

   A relying party MUST NOT accept a Participant Document unless one of
   the following holds:

   1.  A "kyb" or "tax-registration" attestation from an issuer on an
       accepted trust list binds the PID to the key that signs the
       document.

   2.  The "_itp" record of the participant's domain was validated with
       DNSSEC and its "kt" field lists the key that signs the document,
       and a "domain-control" attestation from an accepted issuer binds
       that domain and that key to the PID.

   3.  The document is signed with a key bound to an X.509 certificate
       [RFC5280] that identifies the participant, is carried with its
       chain as "x5c" of the signing key's JWK in "jwks", and chains to
       a trust anchor carried as "x5c" of a key in the "jwks" of an
       "attestation-issuer" entry of an accepted trust list, for example
       a qualified certificate for electronic seals under [EIDAS2], a
       certificate issued under the electronic signature law of another
       jurisdiction, or a certificate issued to the taxpayer by a
       certification authority that a tax authority runs or licenses for
       invoicing.  Trust anchors are local configuration and can include
       certification authorities accredited in only one jurisdiction.
       Some certificates identify a billing device or software
       installation rather than the taxpayer; local configuration or the
       applicable profile states which certificate profiles identify the
       participant.

   Rules 1 and 3 are of higher assurance than rule 2.  When a relying
   party holds an accepted document for a PID and obtains another one:

   *  A document accepted under rule 2 MUST NOT replace one accepted
      under rule 1 or rule 3.

   *  A document that adds a key not present in the accepted document
      MUST be signed with a non-delegated key of the accepted document
      (Section 6.4), or the attestation that covers the new key MUST
      carry a Receipt from a transparency service in an accepted trust
      list.  In the second case the new key is "recently bound" for 72
      hours after the registration time of that Receipt.

   *  A document that omits a key present in the accepted document MUST
      be rejected.

   *  Otherwise, the document with the greater "seq" replaces the other.
      If two different documents have the same "seq", the one registered
      first in a transparency service accepted by the relying party
      prevails.  If neither is registered, the relying party MUST NOT
      deliver to that participant through ITP until a document with a
      greater "seq" is accepted.

   A key bound to a PID for the first time through an attestation
   registered less than 72 hours earlier is also "recently bound" until
   72 hours after registration; this rule applies to first contact.  A
   key that a relying party accepts for the first time under rule 3 is
   recently bound until 72 hours after the "notBefore" time of its
   certificate.  Participants accepted under rule 3 SHOULD register
   their Participant Documents (Section 6.5), since certificates are not
   registered in ITP transparency services.  Companion documents that
   carry payment details, such as [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle], say how
   recently bound keys affect them.

6.3.  Attestations

   Attestations are SD-JWTs [RFC9901] with "typ" "itp-attestation+sd-
   jwt".  The issuer-signed JWT contains "iss" (the "id" of the
   attestation issuer's trust list entry), "sub" (the subject's PID in
   URN form, Section 4), "type" (from the ITP Attestation Types
   registry), and the claims of that type.  The initial types are:

   kyb:  The entity exists and is active and a representative was
      verified.  Claims: "pids" (all PIDs covered, in URN form) and
      "keys" (thumbprints of the participant keys the entity confirmed).

   tax-registration:  The PID in "sub" is a valid tax identifier of the
      entity.  Claims: "keys", and "check" with the source and date of
      the check, for example a query to the validation service of a tax
      authority or to a service that covers several jurisdictions, such
      as [VIES].

   domain-control:  The legal entity identified by "sub" controls the
      domain in "domain", as verified by the attestation issuer both for
      the domain (for example with a technique from
      [I-D.ietf-dnsop-domain-verification-techniques]) and for the
      entity.  Claims: "domain" and "keys".  Its validity period is at
      most 90 days.

   issuance-authorization:  The seller in "sub" authorizes another party
      to issue documents on its behalf.  Claims: "authorized_issuer"
      (the authorized PID in URN form), "role" ("buyer" or "third-
      party"), and "period", an object with "start" and optional "end"
      ("full-date" values); the authorization covers documents whose
      "issue_date" falls in that period.

   The attestation type "tax-registration" is distinct from the stamp
   type of the same name (Section 7.6); text that uses the name says
   which one it means.

   Claims not registered by [RFC7519] are private claim names
   (Section 4.3 of [RFC7519]) whose meaning is given by this section for
   the "typ" value "itp-attestation+sd-jwt" only.

   [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle] defines an attestation type for payment
   accounts.  Every attestation includes "iat", "exp", and a status
   reference in the format of [I-D.ietf-oauth-status-list].  Attestation
   issuers MUST register each attestation they issue in a transparency
   service (Section 10), so that participants, or monitors acting for
   them (Section 6.5), can detect attestations issued without their
   knowledge.  An attestation issuer that learns that a key listed in
   "keys" is compromised, or that an attestation was issued in error,
   MUST revoke the attestation through its status reference.  When it
   obtains a Participant Document in order to deliver, a relying party
   uses an attestation under Section 6.2 only if it is within its
   validity period and not revoked at that time.

6.4.  Keys

   The "kid" of each JWK MUST be its JWK SHA-256 Thumbprint [RFC7638],
   and each JWK MUST contain "alg".  A verifier MUST reject a signature
   whose algorithm differs from the "alg" of the key.  The validity of a
   key is given by the "nbf" and "exp" JWK parameters and its revocation
   by the "revoked" JWK parameter, all registered by [OPENID-FED].  A
   key whose "revoked" member has the reason "compromised" is treated as
   compromised from its "revoked_at" time.

   Keys are never removed from "jwks"; a key that is no longer used is
   expired or revoked.  Revocation is monotonic: once a relying party
   has seen a key revoked in an accepted document, it treats the key as
   revoked even if a later document omits the "revoked" member, and it
   keeps the earliest "revoked_at" it has seen.

   Implementations MUST support verification with "Ed25519" [RFC8032]
   [RFC9864], "ES256", and "PS256" [RFC7518].  Ed25519 public keys are
   JWKs with "kty" "OKP" and "crv" "Ed25519" [RFC8037].  Ed25519
   signatures are verified with the checks of Section 5.1.7 of
   [RFC8032], rejecting non-canonical encodings.  Every envelope MUST
   carry at least one "issuer" or "delegated-issuer" signature with one
   of these algorithms.  An issuer MAY add signatures with other
   algorithms, including ML-DSA as specified for JOSE in [RFC9964]; the
   envelope core lists every algorithm used in "sig_algs" (Section 7.2).
   Keys certified for tax purposes can use other algorithms, such as
   RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5 ("RS256") on hardware tokens, or national
   algorithms such as GOST R 34.10-2012 [RFC7091] or SM2 with SM3.  A
   signature with such a key is added as an additional signature, once a
   JOSE identifier exists for its algorithm, or is applied inside the
   legal original and declared in "native_signature" (Section 7.3); the
   envelope still needs a signature with one of the mandatory
   algorithms, which can be a "delegated-issuer" signature, so that any
   verifier can check it.  The values "none" and "EdDSA", and symmetric
   algorithms, MUST be rejected [RFC8725].  Implementations SHOULD
   support verification with "RS256" [RFC7518], which keys certified for
   tax purposes often use.

   A signature is valid only if the key was within its validity period
   at the signing time, and the signing time precedes any compromise
   time.  The signing time is established by a Receipt (Section 10) or
   by a timestamp stamp; a time asserted by the signer alone is not
   sufficient.  Where the profile for its scheme allows it, a "fiscal-
   device-signature", "tax-registration", or "tax-clearance" stamp from
   a signer accepted under Section 7.6 also establishes it; this serves
   issuers whose certified devices or tax platforms are reachable when a
   transparency service is not.  When neither is yet available, a
   relying party MAY use the time at which it received the envelope,
   provided the key is valid and not revoked at that time, and it re-
   evaluates the signature when evidence is added later.

   To change keys, the participant publishes a Participant Document with
   a greater "seq" in the General JSON Serialization, signed with both
   the old and the new non-delegated key, so that it proves possession
   of both, as ACME account key rollover does (Section 7.3.5 of
   [RFC8555]).  If the old key is compromised, an attestation issuer
   instead attests the new key (Section 6.2).

6.5.  Key Transparency and Monitoring

   Each Participant Document SHOULD be registered in a transparency
   service (Section 10).  Participants, or monitors acting for them, can
   then detect keys or attestations that an operator or attestation
   issuer added without their consent.  This is a simpler form of the
   key transparency systems described in
   [I-D.ietf-keytrans-architecture]: registrations are logged for
   monitoring, without the verifiable lookups that those systems
   provide.

   A participant can rely on a monitor: a service that watches the logs
   for registrations that name its PIDs or keys and alerts the
   participant.  Monitors have no role in the protocol, and any party
   can operate one.

7.  The Envelope

7.1.  Serialization

   An envelope is a JWS in the General JSON Serialization [RFC7515],
   with one additional member, "itp_evidence":

   {
     "payload": "<base64url of JCS(envelope core)>",
     "signatures": [
       {"protected": "<base64url of header>",
        "signature": "<base64url of signature>"}
     ],
     "itp_evidence": {
       "renditions": [],
       "stamps": [],
       "receipts": []
     }
   }

   The payload is the octet string that JCS produces from the envelope
   core.  Verifiers check signatures over the payload octets as received
   and MUST NOT re-serialize the core before verification.  Signatures
   MUST NOT have an unprotected "header" member.  "itp_evidence" is not
   covered by the issuer signature; each item in it is a separately
   signed object bound to the envelope digest.

   The envelope digest is the SHA-256 hash of the payload octets.  Where
   this document writes a digest as a JSON value, it is an object with
   "alg" and "value" (the base64url-encoded hash).  Values of "alg" are
   taken from the "Hash Algorithms for HTTP Digest Fields" registry
   [RFC9530]; "sha-256" MUST be supported.

   Over HTTP, an envelope uses the media type "application/itp-
   envelope+jws" (Section 17.3).

7.2.  Envelope Core

   Members are listed with their requirement level and JSON type.  The
   correspondence with [EN16931] and PINT business terms is given in
   Appendix A for information; mappings to other models are left to
   profiles.

   itp (required, string):  The major version, "1".

   crit (optional, array of strings):  Names of members of the core that
      a receiver MUST understand.  A receiver that does not understand a
      listed member rejects the envelope with "unsupportedExtension".
      Members defined in this document are never listed.

   id (required, string):  A UUIDv7 assigned by the issuing node.

   type (required, string):  A value from the ITP Document Types
      registry.  The initial values are "invoice", "credit-note",
      "debit-note", "prepayment-invoice", "simplified-invoice",
      "consolidated-invoice", "sales-receipt", "payment-receipt" (a
      fiscal document that records a payment, such as a Mexican CFDI of
      type P), "withholding-certificate", "transport-document",
      "correction-letter", and "proforma".  All of them except
      "proforma" are fiscal documents.  A "proforma" document has no
      fiscal effect and MUST NOT carry stamps of type "tax-
      registration", "tax-clearance", or "tax-report".  Table 5 gives
      the properties of each initial value.

   type_code (optional, string):  The [UNTDID1001] document name code,
      for example "380" or "381".  It SHOULD be present where such a
      code applies to the document.

   native_type (optional, object):  The document type code of the
      applicable regime, with "scheme" (from the ITP Bridge Schemes
      registry) and "code", where numbering depends on it, as when
      sequences run per national document type.  Duplicate detection
      (Section 8.4) treats documents with different "native_type" as
      different.

   issuer_role (required, string):  "seller", "buyer" (self-billing), or
      "third-party".

   issuer (required, string):  The PID of the party that issues the
      document and signs the envelope.

   seller (required, string):  The PID of the seller.

   buyer (string):  The PID of the buyer.  It is omitted only for
      documents addressed to consumers or to unidentified buyers.
      Generic identifiers that some tax authorities prescribe for
      consumers, unidentified buyers, or foreign parties identify no
      party; they MUST NOT be used as PIDs and appear only in the
      document.

   party_ids (optional, object):  Additional PIDs of the parties, as
      arrays under "seller" and "buyer", for jurisdictions where a party
      has several tax identifiers.

   recipient (string):  The PID of the party the envelope is delivered
      to: normally the buyer, and the seller in self-billing.  Required
      when the envelope is delivered through ITP.

   authorization (string):  Required when "issuer_role" is not "seller"
      and the document is issued on the seller's behalf: an "issuance-
      authorization" attestation (Section 6.3) in which the seller
      authorizes the issuer.  Document types that the buyer issues in
      its own right, such as "withholding-certificate", are marked as
      such in the ITP Document Types registry; they need no
      authorization, and "issuer" MUST equal "buyer".

   issuer_node (required, string):  The URL of the issuing node's Node
      Document.  Status messages from the recipient's side are sent to
      it (Section 8.8).

   issuer_participant_document (optional, string):  The HTTPS URL of the
      issuer's current Participant Document, so that a recipient that
      has not dealt with the issuer before can obtain it.  It carries no
      trust: the document is accepted only under Section 6.2, and the
      rules for dereferencing URLs in Section 8 apply.  It SHOULD be
      present.

   invoice_number (string):  The identifier that the issuer assigned to
      the document, including any series, unique within that series.
      Required for fiscal documents unless the number is assigned by a
      tax authority, in which case it appears in "fiscal_codes" of the
      core or of a stamp or rendition (Section 7.6).

   issue_date (required, string):  The date of issue as stated in the
      document, a "full-date".

   issue_tz (optional, string):  The time zone name from the IANA Time
      Zone Database [RFC6557] in which "issue_date" is expressed.  It
      SHOULD be present when the issuer's country has more than one time
      zone or the issuer is not the seller.  If it is absent, the time
      zone of the place of issue stated in the document applies or, if
      the document states none, verifiers treat "issue_date" as
      uncertain by one day when they compare it with other times.

   issued_at (optional, string):  The "date-time" at which the document
      was generated, where a jurisdiction gives legal effect to a time
      of issue.

   issuance_mode (optional, string):  "normal" (the default),
      "contingency" when the document was issued under a procedure that
      the tax authority defines for periods in which its system cannot
      be reached, or "offline" when the issuing device or software was
      not connected.  Both require later regularization under the
      applicable rules.

   tax_point_date (optional, string):  The date on which the tax becomes
      chargeable, if different from the issue date.

   invoice_period (optional, object):  "start" and "end" dates of a
      summary or periodic invoice.

   currency (required, string):  The [ISO4217] code of the invoice
      currency.

   tax_currency (optional, string):  The [ISO4217] code in which tax is
      accounted, when it differs from "currency".

   exchange_rate (optional, object):  "rate" (a decimal string, the
      number of units of "tax_currency" per unit of "currency"), "date",
      and "source", when amounts are converted for tax purposes.

   total_with_tax, amount_due (strings):  The invoice total including
      taxes, and the amount due for payment, as "amount" values.
      Required for document types that the ITP Document Types registry
      marks as stating an amount, and omitted otherwise, for example in
      correction letters and transport documents.

   tax_summary (optional, array):  One object per tax, component,
      category, and jurisdiction, with "scheme" (for example "vat",
      "gst", "sales-tax", or a national scheme registered in the ITP Tax
      Schemes registry), optional "component" (for taxes split between
      levels of government, such as the central, state, and integrated
      components of Indian GST, as the profile names them), "category",
      "percent" (a percentage as a decimal string), "base", and
      "amount"; optional "jurisdiction" (an [ISO3166] or [ISO3166-2]
      code, or a code that the tax scheme defines for a county, city, or
      municipality); and optional "withheld" (true when the buyer
      withholds the tax and pays it to the authority, so that it is not
      part of "amount_due").  A tax levied per unit has "unit_amount"
      and "unit" in place of "percent".  A verifier can use it to
      compare tax totals with tax authority records without parsing the
      document.

   fiscal_codes (optional, array):  Codes that identify the document for
      tax purposes, each with "scheme" (from the ITP Bridge Schemes
      registry), "type" ("number", "uuid", "hash", or a value that the
      specification of the bridge scheme defines), "value",
      "assigned_by" ("issuer", "authority", or "device" for a certified
      fiscal device or control unit), and optional "qr_payload".  Codes
      assigned by an authority are usually not known when the core is
      signed; they are then carried in the stamp or rendition that the
      authority's response produces (Section 7.6).

   chain (optional, object):  For regimes that chain documents: "scope"
      (the series, device, or establishment the chain belongs to),
      "counter", and "prev_digest" (a digest object over the previous
      document as the regime defines it).

   fiscal_device (optional, object):  For documents produced by a
      certified fiscal device: "scheme" (from the ITP Bridge Schemes
      registry), "serial", and "registration_id".

   native_signature (optional, object):  Declares a signature or seal
      inside the document that the applicable regime requires: "format"
      (for example "xades", "cades", "jades", "xmldsig" for XML
      Signature without AdES properties, or a seal format defined by a
      bridge scheme, such as a signature over a canonical string of the
      document), "level" (where the format defines levels), and
      "cert_scheme" (the certification framework of the signing
      certificate, such as a national public key infrastructure or
      certificates issued by a tax authority).  The signature itself
      stays in the document (Section 7.3).

   document_languages (optional, array of strings):  Language tags
      [RFC5646] of the human-readable text in the document.

   profiles (optional, array of strings):  Identifiers of the profiles
      that apply to the document, from the ITP Profiles registry.  They
      tell the recipient which jurisdiction rules, such as deadlines and
      cancellation policies, apply.

   delivery_role (required, string):  "primary" or "copy" (Section 5.2).

   document (required, object):  The document, given as "content_b64"
      (base64url of its octets) or "href" (an HTTPS URL), together with
      "media_type", optional "customization" and "profile" identifiers,
      and "digest", computed over the octets.  "content_b64" is
      RECOMMENDED.  An "href" URL MUST be same-origin with "issuer_node"
      or with a node listed in "senders" of the issuer's Participant
      Document.  A recipient that receives "href" MUST retrieve the
      document when it accepts the envelope and store it with the
      envelope.  JSON documents are also carried as octets, so the
      digest never depends on canonicalization.

   attachments (optional, array):  Supporting documents, each with
      "name", "media_type", "digest", and "content_b64" or "href",
      processed like "document".

   legal_original (string):  "document", or "rendition" when the legal
      original is produced by a clearance channel (Section 7.3).
      Required for fiscal documents.

   required_evidence (optional, array of strings):  Stamp types
      (Section 7.6) that the issuer has obtained or will obtain for this
      envelope.  Verifiers report any that is missing.

   sig_algs (required, array of strings):  The algorithms of all issuer
      and delegated-issuer signatures that the issuer applied.  For each
      algorithm in "sig_algs" that a verifier supports, it MUST find a
      valid issuer or delegated-issuer signature with that algorithm, so
      that removing a signature cannot downgrade verification.  An
      "issuer" or "delegated-issuer" signature whose algorithm is not
      listed in "sig_algs" is reported as failed and is not used to
      validate the envelope.

   references (optional, object):  "order", "buyer_reference",
      "contract", "project", "receiving_advice", "despatch_advice" (an
      array), "prepayments" (an array of references to prepayment
      documents settled by this one), "public_buyer" (an object with
      administrative codes of a public buyer, as a profile defines
      them), and "replaces", the id of an envelope that this one
      replaces after a clearance rejection.

   correction (optional, object):  For corrective documents: "corrects",
      an array of references to corrected documents, each an object with
      "envelope_id", with "invoice_number" and "issue_date", or, for a
      document whose number an authority assigned, with "fiscal_code",
      an object with "scheme", "type", and "value" as in "fiscal_codes";
      "method" ("substitution" or "difference"); optional "period" for
      corrections that cover a period; and "code", a code of the
      applicable jurisdiction.

   payment (optional, object):  Payment terms, as defined in
      [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle].  A receiver that does not implement
      that document ignores it.

   transparency_salt (string):  32 random octets, base64url-encoded.
      Required if the envelope is registered (Section 10).

   agent (optional, object):  Present when a software agent produced the
      envelope; defined in [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates].

   Members that repeat content of the document MUST be consistent with
   it, and the document prevails.  A receiving node that interprets the
   document and finds a contradiction rejects the envelope with
   "inconsistentCore".  XML documents MUST be parsed with document type
   definitions and external entities disabled.

7.3.  Legal Original

   Where the issuer's own document is the invoice, the legal original is
   "document".  In clearance regimes, the invoice is the artifact that
   the tax authority, or a provider it accredits, accepts, stamps, or
   delivers.  A bridge adds that artifact to "itp_evidence.renditions".
   Because the core is signed before the rendition exists, the issuer
   sets "legal_original" to "rendition", and the verifier takes as legal
   original the rendition from a signer accepted under Section 7.6 for
   the applicable scheme.  If there is more than one such rendition for
   the same scheme, the verifier reports the conflict and does not
   choose.  The envelope core is not modified after signing; renditions
   are carried only in "itp_evidence".

   A human-readable rendition, such as a PDF produced from the document,
   is never the legal original unless the applicable profile says so.
   Renditions produced for people are expected to be accessible, for
   example by following [WCAG22] for HTML renditions.

   Where the tax authority's platform issues the invoice and assigns its
   number, the data that the issuer submits is not an invoice until the
   platform issues it.  The issuer sets "legal_original" to "rendition"
   and omits "invoice_number", and a bridge adds the platform's output
   as a rendition.  The same applies where the law makes a code added by
   the authority part of the invoice.

   The issuer signature is an envelope-level signature.  It is not by
   itself an advanced or qualified electronic signature or seal under
   [EIDAS2] or under any other regime.  A "delegated-issuer" signature
   is made by an operator with a key that the participant lists in
   "delegates" of its Participant Document: it shows that the
   participant authorized that operator to issue in its name, not that
   the participant itself applied the signature.  Whether ITP evidence
   contributes to the controls that establish authenticity of origin and
   integrity of an invoice under the applicable tax law, for example
   Article 233 of [EU-VAT-DIR] in the European Union, is for the issuer
   and its tax authority to determine.  Where a signature or seal on the
   invoice itself is required, it is applied in the native syntax of the
   legal original, for example XAdES [XADES] on UBL, CAdES [CADES], or
   JAdES [JADES], and is declared in "native_signature".

7.4.  Conversion by Service Providers

   Service providers often convert an issuer's data into the syntax that
   the recipient receives, or add data to it.  ITP does not make such a
   converted document second to the issuer's own.

   *  Conversion before signing.  When the issuer's provider converts or
      enriches the data before the envelope is signed, the converted
      document is the "document" of the envelope.  The provider's node
      signs it with a "delegated-issuer" key, or returns it to the
      issuer for an "issuer" signature.  Either signature is checked by
      any later holder like any other, and the delegated key shows which
      operator the issuer authorized (Section 7.3).

   *  Conversion after delivery.  When the recipient's node converts the
      document into the syntax its participant uses, the conversion is
      local processing and needs no signature or trust list.  The node
      keeps the envelope, so that the participant and later holders can
      verify the signed document from which the conversion was made.

   *  Conversion in transit.  A node that converts an envelope signed by
      the issuer before delivery cannot change the signed document.  It
      either delivers the envelope unchanged, or adds the converted
      document as a rendition, which is not the legal original unless a
      profile says so (Section 7.6).

7.5.  Signature Header Parameters

   The protected header of every signature on an envelope contains
   "alg", "kid", "typ" with the value "itp-envelope+jws", "itp_role",
   and "crit" with a value that includes "itp_role" (Section 4.1.11 of
   [RFC7515]).  A JOSE library that does not know "itp_role" therefore
   rejects the signature instead of ignoring the role.

   itp_role:  A value from the ITP Signer Roles registry: "issuer" (a
      key of the participant that signs the object: the issuer of an
      envelope, or the party in "by" of a status message), "delegated-
      issuer" (a key listed in "delegates" of that participant's
      Participant Document), "node" (a node key, for the statuses that a
      node signs), "operator-seal" (a node stating that it validated the
      envelope), "bridge" (a key of a bridge, for renditions, stamps,
      and the statuses a bridge emits), or "agent" (see
      [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates]).

   A verifier MUST reject an "issuer" signature whose key is listed in
   "delegates", and a "delegated-issuer" signature whose key is not.  An
   envelope is valid if at least one "issuer" or "delegated-issuer"
   signature verifies with a key valid at the signing time, and the
   "sig_algs" rule in Section 7.2 is met.  Other signatures that fail
   are reported and ignored, so that a signature added by a third party
   cannot invalidate an envelope.  An "operator-seal" or "agent"
   signature is never accepted in place of an "issuer" or "delegated-
   issuer" signature.  An "operator-seal" signature is made with a key
   listed in "kids" of the "senders" entry for that node in the issuer's
   Participant Document or, on a status message, in the Participant
   Document of the party in "by".  Keys for "agent" signatures come from
   the mandate in the "agent" member, and not from Section 6; a verifier
   that does not implement [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates] ignores
   "agent" signatures.

   A protected header MAY contain "x5c" when the key is accepted under
   rule 3 of Section 6.2.  A key in "jwk" or "x5c" MUST NOT be used
   unless its JWK SHA-256 Thumbprint equals a "kid" accepted under
   Section 6 for the signing time.

7.6.  Evidence Objects

   Each rendition and stamp is a JWS in the Flattened JSON Serialization
   signed by the bridge that obtained it from the authority, network,
   fiscal device, or timestamping service, or by the issuer's node where
   the profile for the scheme lets that node act as bridge.  Its
   protected header has "typ" "itp-rendition+jws" or "itp-stamp+jws",
   "itp_role" "bridge", and "crit" including "itp_role".  Any signature
   of the authority, device, or service itself is carried in
   "evidence_token".  The JWS payload contains "type" (required, a stamp
   type, or "rendition"), "scheme" (required, from the ITP Bridge
   Schemes registry), "envelope_digest" (required), "issued_at"
   (required), optional "authority_reference" (the identifier that the
   authority or network assigned), optional "response_digest" (the
   digest of the authority's or network's response), optional
   "evidence_token" (base64url of a token that the scheme defines), and
   optional "fiscal_codes" with the same structure as in Section 7.2.  A
   "timestamp" stamp has the scheme "rfc3161" and carries in
   "evidence_token" a time-stamp token [RFC3161] over the envelope
   digest or, in a status message, over the status message digest.  A
   verifier uses the genTime of the token as a trusted time only if the
   token validates to a time-stamping authority accepted by local
   configuration or whose certificate is for a key in the "jwks" of the
   bridge's trust list entry, and its messageImprint equals, under the
   same hash algorithm, the envelope digest or, for a stamp in the
   "itp_evidence" of a status message, the status message digest
   (Section 9).  A rendition also contains "media_type" and
   "content_b64" with the legal original.  For example:

   {
     "type": "tax-registration",
     "scheme": "example-registration",
     "envelope_digest": {"alg": "sha-256",
       "value": "<envelope digest>"},
     "issued_at": "2026-10-28T10:15:03Z",
     "authority_reference": "A-0000000000000000",
     "response_digest": {"alg": "sha-256",
       "value": "RBNvo1WzZ4oRRq0W9-hknpT7T8If536DEMBg9hyq_4o"}
   }

   Stamp types and scheme names are registered (Section 17.7).  The
   initial stamp types are "tax-registration", "tax-clearance", "tax-
   report", "network-ack", "fiscal-device-signature", and "timestamp".
   The bridge retains the response whose digest is in "response_digest".
   Verifiers accept a rendition or a stamp only if its signer is a
   bridge on an accepted trust list for the scheme concerned, a node
   listed in "senders" of the issuer's Participant Document when the
   profile for that scheme allows the issuer's own node to act as
   bridge, or a bridge whose key the verifier accepts by local
   configuration, for example the access point of a network the verifier
   already uses.  Unknown stamp types are reported and otherwise
   ignored.

   Stamps can also be attached to envelopes that are never delivered,
   for example when a jurisdiction requires reporting of a transaction
   but no exchange of the invoice through ITP.

   Receipts are described in Section 10.

8.  HTTP Transport

8.1.  General

   ITP uses HTTPS with TLS 1.3 [RFC9846], following [RFC9325], and
   servers are authenticated as described in [RFC9525].  The TLS trust
   anchors that a node uses are local configuration and need not be
   limited to those of web browsers.  Nodes MAY also offer TLS 1.3
   cipher suites with national algorithms, such as those of [RFC8998] or
   [RFC9367], but MUST support the mandatory-to-implement cipher suite,
   signature algorithms, and key exchange groups of Section 9.1 of
   [RFC9846], so that nodes in different jurisdictions can connect.
   HTTP semantics follow [RFC9110].  Clients obtain endpoint URLs from
   Node Documents.

   When a node dereferences a URL while processing ITP objects,
   including status list and trust list URLs, it MUST use HTTPS, MUST
   NOT follow redirects to other origins, and MUST apply limits on size,
   time, and the number of retrievals caused by one inbound request.  It
   MUST check the address it actually connects to, after DNS resolution
   and for every connection, and MUST NOT connect to addresses whose
   "Globally Reachable" value is "False" in the IANA special-purpose
   address registries [IANA-SPECIAL] [RFC6890] [RFC8190], such as
   loopback, private, or link-local addresses.  This prevents DNS
   rebinding.  A node MUST NOT obtain verification keys from URLs found
   in an inbound envelope; keys come only from documents accepted under
   Section 6.

8.2.  Request Authentication and Replay Protection

   Every request between nodes, and every response that a node generates
   for such a request, MUST be signed with HTTP Message Signatures
   [RFC9421], in a profile similar to the one in
   [I-D.ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol]:

   *  The signature label is "itp" and the "tag" parameter is "itp".

   *  The "keyid" parameter is the "kid" of a key in the sending node's
      Node Document.  The algorithm is determined by that key and not by
      an "alg" parameter.  Node keys are "Ed25519" keys, used with
      "ed25519", or "ES256" keys, used with "ecdsa-p256-sha256".

   *  Requests with content cover "@method", "@target-uri", "content-
      digest" [RFC9530], "content-type", and "idempotency-key".
      Requests without content cover "@method" and "@target-uri".  All
      requests include the "created", "expires", and "nonce" parameters,
      and "expires" is at most 300 seconds after "created".

   *  Responses cover "@status" and, if there is content, "content-
      digest", plus the request components "@method", "@target-uri",
      and, where present, "idempotency-key", with the "req" parameter
      (Section 2.4 of [RFC9421]).  They include the "created" and
      "keyid" parameters.

   *  Requests to the "nonce" endpoint use HEAD or GET and are not
      signed, since the client has no nonce yet.

   A client MUST verify the signature of each response with a key listed
   in "kids" of the "inbound" or "senders" entry, or for copies
   (Section 8.5) the "archive" entry, through which it found that node.
   A client treats an unsigned response, or one whose signature does not
   verify, such as a 413 or 502 response from an intermediary, as a
   transport failure and retries according to Section 8.4.

   A receiver MUST verify "content-digest" against the content received
   and MUST reject a signature whose "created" time is more than 60
   seconds in the future.  A request whose HTTP Message Signature or
   "content-digest" does not verify is rejected with "badSignature".

   Nonces are issued by the receiving node and carried in the Replay-
   Nonce response field defined in Section 6.5.1 of [RFC8555].  A client
   obtains a first nonce from the "nonce" endpoint, and every response
   from a node includes a fresh one.  A node MUST accept each nonce at
   most once and MUST reject a request with an unknown or used nonce
   with the error "badNonce" (Section 8.7).  Nonces SHOULD be generated
   so that the node can check them without keeping state for unused
   nonces, for example by authenticating an expiry time with a node
   secret, so that the nonce endpoint cannot be used to exhaust memory.

   For delivery, the sending node MUST be listed in "senders" of the
   issuer's Participant Document.  For status messages, it MUST be
   listed in "senders" of the issuer's or the recipient's Participant
   Document, or be the node of the recipient's "itp" "inbound" entry
   that accepted the envelope; the status message itself is verified
   separately, as Section 9 describes.  The receiving node resolves
   "keyid" among the "kids" of those entries, and otherwise rejects the
   request with "unauthorizedSender".

   How software authenticates to its own node is outside this
   specification; OAuth 2.0 [RFC6749] with sender-constrained access
   tokens ([RFC9449] or [RFC8705]), as recommended in [RFC9700], is one
   option.

8.3.  Delivery

   A node delivers an envelope with a POST request to the "inbox"
   endpoint of the recipient's node.  It takes that endpoint only from a
   Node Document that it has verified with a key listed in "kids" of the
   recipient's "inbound" entry for that node, so that a party that
   controls only the node's web origin, for example after its domain has
   expired, does not receive envelopes.  The recipient is the
   "recipient" member of the signed core.  The values below are those of
   Appendix C; the content, shown truncated, is the 1365-octet envelope
   described there:

   NOTE: '\' line wrapping per RFC 8792

   POST /itp/in HTTP/1.1
   Host: node.example.net
   Content-Type: application/itp-envelope+jws
   Content-Length: 1365
   Idempotency-Key: "01a18201-84a0-7b2a-9d41-6c1f0e8a5b77"
   Content-Digest: \
     sha-256=:tGM8fHYi3ZCow6CDlj142ehZqwBn0THA+Lw0u7U3zMc=:
   Signature-Input: itp=("@method" "@target-uri" "content-digest" \
     "content-type" "idempotency-key");created=1793182503;\
     expires=1793182803;nonce="dG9rZW4tMDAx";\
     keyid="FtIu-VbGrfe_KB6CH7GNwODB72MNxj_ml11dEvO-7kk";tag="itp"
   Signature: itp=:bOzCUemhJyw3eBk8Wy/00zxlzv773K0IBmEaFL5e6LkQqP2W8rDR\
     dAK9uujlKF8hzCc1KwTj57eziXc3ysvjDA==:

   {"itp_evidence":{"receipts":[],"renditions":[],"stamps":[]},...}

   The receiving node validates the request and the envelope as in steps
   1 to 4 of Section 11.  If it accepts the envelope, it responds with
   status 201, a Location field with the envelope URL, and content of
   type "application/itp-status+jws" holding a "delivered" status
   message.  Otherwise the response is a problem details object
   [RFC9457] with an ITP error type, for example "unknownRecipient" when
   the node serves no participant with the PID in "recipient", or
   "untrustedIssuer" when step 2 of Section 11 fails.  An envelope
   larger than "max_envelope_size" results in status 413 with
   "envelopeTooLarge".  A receiving node that learns, after a 201
   response, that it cannot pass the envelope to the recipient sends a
   "technical-rejected" status (Section 9).

   A receiving node processes a delivery request in this order: it
   checks the size, the nonce, and "content-digest"; it performs step 1
   of Section 11; it obtains and accepts the issuer's Participant
   Document as in step 2; it resolves "keyid" among the "kids" of the
   issuer's "senders" entries and verifies the HTTP Message Signature
   (Section 8.2); it completes steps 2 and 3; and only then does it look
   up the recipient and perform step 4.  The time it takes to fail one
   of the checks before the recipient lookup does not depend on the
   recipient.  It returns "unknownRecipient" only to a sending node that
   has passed these checks, and it rate-limits such answers per issuer.
   A client that has not passed them therefore cannot learn from the
   inbox whether a PID is a participant of the node.

8.4.  Idempotency and Duplicates

   ITP uses the Idempotency-Key field defined in
   [I-D.ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header] for its syntax and
   registration; the processing rules below replace those of that
   document for ITP requests.  The field is required on delivery and
   status requests.  Its value is the id of the envelope or status
   message.  The scope of a key is the authenticated sending node.  Two
   requests have the same content if they carry the same envelope
   digest, for envelopes, or the same status message digest, so a retry
   that adds evidence to "itp_evidence" is not a conflict.  The
   receiving node verifies new evidence items, adds them to its stored
   envelope, and returns them on a GET request to "envelope".  A missing
   key results in status 400 with "idempotencyKeyMissing", reuse of a
   key with different content in status 422 with "idempotencyKeyReused",
   and a concurrent request with the same key in status 409 with
   "requestInProgress".  A repeated request with the same content
   receives the original response.  Nodes MUST retain keys for at least
   7 days.

   Independently of HTTP idempotency, a receiving node keeps track of
   the envelopes it has received for as long as it retains them:

   *  An envelope with the same envelope digest as one already received
      is a duplicate.  The node returns the original "delivered" status
      and MUST NOT present it to the recipient as a new payable.

   *  An envelope with an "id" already seen and a different digest is
      rejected with "envelopeInvalid", unless the Idempotency-Key rules
      already reject it with "idempotencyKeyReused".

   *  An envelope with a different "id" from one already received, but
      with the same issuer (any PID verified for the same Participant
      Document) and the same "invoice_number" within the same numbering
      scope, where both envelopes contain "invoice_number" and either
      neither contains "native_type" or both contain equal "native_type"
      values, or with a "fiscal_codes" entry assigned by an authority or
      a device (in the core or in an accepted stamp or rendition) that
      has the same "scheme", "type", and "value", is delivered and
      flagged to the recipient as a possible duplicate, unless its
      "references.replaces" names an envelope that was rejected in
      clearance.  The numbering scope is defined by the applicable
      profile, for example a fiscal year from 1 April to 31 March;
      without a profile it is the calendar year of "issue_date".

8.5.  Copies for the Issuer

   A participant can name in "archive" of its Participant Document a
   node that keeps a copy of every envelope issued in its name
   (Section 5.5).  When the issuer's accepted Participant Document
   contains "archive", a node listed in its "senders" that creates an
   envelope MUST send a copy of it to the archive node, whether or not
   it delivers the envelope through ITP, unless it is itself that node.
   The copy is the envelope as signed, with the evidence the sending
   node holds.  It is sent with a POST request to the "copies" endpoint
   of the archive node, which the sending node takes only from a Node
   Document that it has verified with a key listed in "kids" of the
   "archive" entry.  Section 8.2 and Section 8.3 apply to the request
   and the response, with these differences:

   *  The archive node accepts a copy only if the sending node is listed
      in "senders" of the issuer's accepted Participant Document and the
      archive node is the node named in "archive" of that document;
      otherwise it rejects the request with "unauthorizedSender".  It
      validates the envelope as in steps 1 to 4 of Section 11.  Where
      the processing order of Section 8.3 looks up the recipient, the
      archive node looks up the participant named in "issuer", and it
      does not answer "unknownRecipient"; "recipient" can be absent from
      a copy.

   *  The envelope is not changed for the copy.  Its "delivery_role"
      keeps the meaning given in Section 5.2 and refers to delivery to
      the recipient.  What marks the request as a copy is the endpoint
      to which it is sent, which the HTTP Message Signature covers.  The
      archive node therefore holds an envelope with the same envelope
      digest as the one the recipient holds.

   *  On success the archive node responds with status 201 and a
      Location field, without content.  It signs no status message about
      a copy, so a copy never produces a "delivered" or "technical-
      rejected" status.

   *  Idempotency keys of requests to "copies" have a scope of their
      own, separate from that of requests to "inbox" and "status" at the
      same node.  The duplicate rules of Section 8.4 apply among the
      copies held for one participant, separately from the envelopes
      received for it: a copy with an envelope digest already held
      receives the original response, and new evidence is added to the
      stored copy.  A possible duplicate is flagged to the participant,
      which can show that two of its senders used the same number.

   The sending node also sends to the "copies" endpoint, with media type
   "application/itp-status+jws", each status message for the envelope
   that it signs or receives, including the "delivered" status from the
   recipient's node and messages signed by the counterparty.  The
   archive node stores each status message whose signature is valid with
   the copy named by its "envelope_id" and "envelope_digest", and
   answers 404 for an envelope it does not hold.  It does not send
   copies or their status messages to any other node; the forwarding
   rule of Section 8.8 does not apply to them.  It presents copies to
   the participant as documents issued in the participant's name and
   MUST NOT present them as documents received from another party.

8.6.  Asynchronous Results

   Bridges to clearance platforms can take minutes or hours to produce a
   result.  A node that cannot complete processing synchronously
   responds with status 202, a Location field identifying the envelope,
   and a Retry-After field.  The client obtains the result with a signed
   GET request to that location.  Until processing completes, the node
   answers again with status 202 and Retry-After; afterwards it answers
   with status 200 and the "delivered" status message, or with the
   problem details object that the POST request would have received.

8.7.  Errors

   object contains a "subproblems" array whose elements have "type",
   "detail", and, where applicable, "pointer" (a JSON Pointer [RFC6901]
   into the envelope core), as in Section 6.7.1 of [RFC8555].  "detail"
   is text for people; a problem details object MAY contain "lang"

8.8.  Status Messages over HTTP

   Status messages (Section 9) are sent with a POST request to the
   "status" endpoint, expanded with the envelope id, with the same
   authentication and idempotency rules as delivery.  Messages signed by
   the issuer, by a bridge used by the issuer, or, when "issuer_role" is
   "seller", by the payee ([I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle]) go to the node
   that returned the "delivered" status.  All other messages go to the
   issuer's node, found through "issuer_node"; a node MUST NOT send them
   there unless that URL is listed in "senders" of the issuer's accepted
   Participant Document.  A node MUST forward to the counterparty's node
   every status message for the envelope that it holds and that the
   counterparty has not received, including those signed by nodes,
   bridges, or third parties.  A party that is not a participant in the
   envelope, such as an assignee, sends its messages through the
   seller's node, which relays them.  Each node stores every status
   message whose signature is valid and returns all of them on a GET
   request to the "envelope" endpoint, so that both sides hold the same
   set.  Clients MAY send Accept-Language; nodes SHOULD take it into
   account for text they generate and set Content-Language.

8.9.  Caching

   Participant Documents and Node Documents are cacheable; servers
   SHOULD send Cache-Control with a "max-age" of at most 86400 seconds.
   Responses that contain envelopes or status messages are private and
   SHOULD carry "Cache-Control: no-store".

8.10.  Versioning and Extensibility

   The "itp" member of the envelope core carries the major version, and
   the "versions" member of a Node Document lists the versions a node
   accepts; a receiving node rejects an envelope whose "itp" value is
   not listed with "unsupportedVersion".  Receivers MUST ignore unknown
   members in ITP objects, except those listed in "crit", and unknown
   fields in the DNS record.  Each member whose values come from a
   registry says what a receiver does with an unknown value; for
   registries where this document says nothing, an unknown value is
   reported to the user and otherwise ignored.  A change that existing
   receivers cannot safely ignore requires either a "crit" entry or a
   new major version.

9.  Status Messages

   A status message is a JWS with "typ" "itp-status+jws", in the
   Flattened JSON Serialization, or in the General JSON Serialization
   when it carries several signatures (for example with an "agent"
   member, [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates]).  Its protected header
   contains "alg", "kid", "typ", "itp_role", and "crit" including
   "itp_role".  Its payload is:

   {
     "id": "01a18205-1c2e-7f00-8a10-2b3c4d5e6f70",
     "envelope_id": "01a18201-84a0-7b2a-9d41-6c1f0e8a5b77",
     "envelope_digest": {"alg": "sha-256",
       "value": "<envelope digest>"},
     "status": "delivered",
     "effective_date": "2026-10-28",
     "by": "https://node.example.net/itp",
     "seq": 1
   }

   id (required, string):  A UUIDv7 assigned by the signer.

   envelope_id, envelope_digest (required):  The envelope the message is
      about.

   status (required, string):  A value from the ITP Statuses registry.

   reason (optional, object):  "code", from the ITP Status Reason Codes
      registry or, for "technical-rejected", the name of an ITP error
      type (Section 17.6); "text"; and optional "lang".

   effective_date (required, string):  The "full-date" on which the
      event took place, which can differ from the date of the message.

   by (required, string):  The signer: the PID of a participant; the
      Node Document URL of a node, for statuses that a node signs; or
      the "id" of a bridge's trust list entry, for statuses that a
      bridge signs.

   seq (required, integer), prev (object):  "seq" counts the messages of
      one signer for one envelope, starting at 1, and "prev" is the
      digest of that signer's previous message; it is absent from the
      first.

   agent (optional, object):  Present when a software agent produced the
      message ([I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates]).

   A status message MAY have an "itp_evidence" member next to "payload"
   and "signatures", with stamps bound to the status message digest, for
   example a timestamp.  A participant MUST sign the status messages for
   one envelope through a single node, so that its "seq" chain does not
   fork.

   The digest of a status message is the SHA-256 hash of its JWS Payload
   octets.  A status message is signed by the party named in "by": a
   participant with a key covered by its accepted Participant Document,
   including a "delegated-issuer" key; a node with a key listed in
   "kids" of the recipient's "inbound" entry for that node; a bridge
   with a key from its trust list entry.

   This document defines two statuses, both signed by the receiving node
   with "itp_role" "node":

   delivered:  The envelope reached the recipient's node and passed
      steps 1 to 4 of Section 11.

   technical-rejected:  The envelope was refused, or could not be passed
      to the recipient, for technical or regulatory reasons; "reason" is
      required and gives the cause.  It is final for delivery;
      [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle] defines its effect on other statuses.

   [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle] defines further statuses, their signers,
   and how a state is derived from the set of messages.  A node that
   does not implement that document stores and returns such messages but
   does not interpret them.

10.  Transparency

10.1.  Signed Statements

   An issuing node SHOULD register each envelope it signs; without a
   Receipt or a timestamp stamp an envelope has no trusted signing time
   (Section 6.4).  After signing an envelope, the issuing node computes
   the commitment

   C = HMAC-SHA256(transparency_salt, envelope digest octets)

   where the HMAC key is the 32 octets obtained by decoding
   "transparency_salt", and registers C in a transparency service
   [RFC9943] in one of two ways:

   Per envelope:  A Signed Statement, which is a COSE_Sign1 message
      [RFC9052], signed with the key that produced the "issuer" or
      "delegated-issuer" envelope signature.  The COSE algorithm
      corresponds to the "alg" of the key: -19 ("Ed25519") and -9
      ("ESP256") as registered by [RFC9864], and -37 ("PS256") as
      registered by [RFC8230].  Its protected header contains the "kid"
      (the UTF-8 octets of the key's JWK "kid" value), the content type
      "application/itp-commitment", and the CWT Claims header parameter
      [RFC9597] with "iss" set to "urn:ietf:params:oauth:jwk-
      thumbprint:sha-256:" followed by the key's thumbprint [RFC9278],
      and "sub" set to the base64url encoding of C.  The payload is C.

   In batches:  The node collects the commitments it creates during an
      interval of at most 3600 seconds, builds a Merkle tree over them
      as defined in Section 2.1 of [RFC9162] with the commitments as
      leaf inputs in creation order, and registers one Signed Statement
      whose payload is the tree head, with content type "application/
      itp-batch-head", signed with a key listed in "kids" of the
      issuer's "senders" entry for the node, with "iss" set to the Node
      Document URL and "sub" set to the base64url encoding of the tree
      head.  Each envelope receives the inclusion proof of its
      commitment (Section 2.1.3 of [RFC9162]).

   Batched registration is RECOMMENDED because it does not reveal to the
   transparency service, or to readers of the log, how many envelopes
   each issuer key produces (Section 15).

   The node registers the Signed Statement, for example with
   [I-D.ietf-scitt-scrapi], and adds to "itp_evidence.receipts" an
   object with: "statement", the base64url encoding of the Signed
   Statement; "receipt", the base64url encoding of the Receipt
   [RFC9942]; and, in batch mode, "batch", an object with "tree_size",
   "leaf_index", and "path" (the inclusion proof as an array of
   base64url values).  The Signed Statement together with its Receipt is
   a Transparent Statement in the sense of [RFC9943].

   The Registration Policy of a transparency service used for ITP MUST
   verify that the Signed Statement is signed with a key accepted under
   Section 6 for the role concerned (participant, node, or attestation
   issuer).  The submitter provides the Participant Document, Node
   Document, or trust list entry that lists the key; the transparency
   service uses it only for this check and does not register it.  The
   transparency service MUST use the RFC9162_SHA256 verifiable data
   structure of [RFC9942], and MUST include the registration time as the
   "iat" claim of a CWT Claims header parameter [RFC9597] in the
   protected header of each Receipt; that is the "registration time"
   used in this document.  An attestation for which no Receipt is
   available is treated as registered when the relying party first sees
   it.

10.2.  Participant Documents and Attestations

   Participant Documents and attestations are registered in the same
   way, with two differences.  The commitment is the SHA-256 hash,
   without a salt, of the JWS Compact Serialization of the Participant
   Document payload and the signature whose "kid" sorts first in
   lexicographic order, or of the issuer-signed JWT of the attestation.
   The CWT "sub" claim is the "PID digest": the base64url encoding of
   the SHA-256 hash of the US-ASCII octets of the normalized PID in URN
   form, so that a participant or its monitor (Section 6.5) can find
   every registration about a PID without learning other PIDs.  For a
   participant that is a natural person, the PID digest is instead an
   HMAC-SHA256 of the PID in URN form with a secret that the participant
   gives to the attestation issuer and to its monitor, so that the log
   does not let others test guesses of the identifier.

10.3.  Receipts and Their Verification

   A verifier checks a Receipt with the key of a transparency service
   that is listed with the role "transparency-service" in an accepted
   trust list.  Receipts alone do not prevent a transparency service
   from showing different logs to different parties.  Transparency
   services SHOULD therefore publish checkpoints of the same tree that
   their Receipts refer to, cosigned by independent witnesses, for
   example with [C2SP-CHECKPOINT] and [C2SP-WITNESS], and verifiers that
   can check such checkpoints SHOULD keep the latest one they have seen
   for each log and check consistency against it.

10.4.  What Transparency Provides

   Registration shows that an envelope existed no later than the
   registration time, and lets participants or their monitors detect
   keys, attestations, or envelopes signed with a stolen key while they
   watch the log.  Registration does not detect duplicates or check
   invoice content, and a missing registration is not evidence of
   forgery.  Duplicates are detected by recipients and by the
   registration systems of tax authorities.

   Transparency services are not needed for discovery, delivery, or
   verification.  A deployment without them obtains a trusted signing
   time from a "timestamp" stamp (Section 7.6) and loses only the
   detection of mis-issued attestations and of envelopes signed with a
   stolen key.

   Verifiers compare the registration time with "issue_date" and
   "invoice_period".  A gap larger than the issuance deadline of the
   applicable jurisdiction is reported as a risk signal.  Envelopes with
   "issuance_mode" "offline" or "contingency", and envelopes from fiscal
   devices that synchronize later, are registered when connectivity
   returns; for them verifiers compare the earliest trusted time in
   their stamps with the regularization period that the profile gives.
   Issuance deadlines differ between jurisdictions and kinds of
   transaction, for example the ten days that [VIDA] sets for certain
   intra-EU transactions from July 2030; profiles say which deadlines
   apply.

11.  Verification

   Any holder of an envelope can verify it locally:

   1.  Check size limits, I-JSON conformance including the rejections in
       Section 2.1, and the structure of the JWS.

   2.  Obtain the issuer's Participant Document, from a document already
       accepted, from "issuer_participant_document", or from a
       directory, and accept it as described in Section 6.  Check that
       "issuer", and "seller" when "issuer_role" is "seller", are PIDs
       verified (Section 4) for the Participant Document that lists the
       signing key; "aliases" alone do not verify a PID.  If
       "issuer_role" is not "seller", check "authorization", unless the
       ITP Document Types registry marks the document type as issued by
       the buyer in its own right, in which case check that "issuer"
       equals "buyer".  If an "agent" member is present and the verifier
       implements [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates], apply it.

   3.  Verify the signatures as described in Section 7.5, including
       "crit", "typ", "itp_role", and "sig_algs", with keys valid at the
       signing time.

   4.  Retrieve the document if needed and recompute its digest.  A
       verifier that interprets the document syntax also checks that the
       core is consistent with the document (Section 7.2); a receiving
       node that does not interpret the syntax omits this check.

   5.  Recompute C and verify each Receipt and, in batch mode, the
       inclusion proof, and check that the key that signed the batch
       statement is listed in "kids" of a "senders" entry of the
       issuer's Participant Document.  In per-envelope mode, check that
       the Signed Statement is signed with the key of a valid "issuer"
       or "delegated-issuer" signature on the envelope.

   6.  Verify each rendition and stamp, and report any stamp type listed
       in "required_evidence" that is missing.  Where the authority
       offers public verification, query it and check that its record
       matches the issuer, the number or fiscal code, the issue date,
       and, where the document type states an amount, the total.

   7.  Check whether the envelope duplicates one already held.

   8.  Verify the chain of status messages (Section 9).

   A failure in steps 1 to 4 makes the envelope invalid.  A receiving
   node then answers "envelopeInvalid" for steps 1 and 3 and for a
   digest mismatch in step 4, "untrustedIssuer" for step 2, and
   "inconsistentCore" for a contradiction found in step 4 (Section 8.7).
   The results of steps 5 to 8 are reported and do not by themselves
   make the envelope invalid.

   Authenticity is judged with the status of attestations at the signing
   time.  A verification result covers the origin and integrity of an
   envelope.  It does not establish that the issuer is a known supplier
   or that the amount is owed, and is not an authorization to pay.
   Envelopes signed with a recently bound key (Section 6.2) are reported
   as such.  A receiving node also reports to the recipient an envelope
   from an issuer from which that recipient has not received an envelope
   before.

   A node MAY offer verification as a service.  The result is a JWS with
   "typ" "itp-verification+jws", signed with a node key, whose payload
   has "envelope_digest", "verified_at", "verifier" (the Node Document
   URL), and "results", an array with one object per step above, each
   with "step" (the step number), "result" ("pass", "fail", or "not-
   applicable"), and optional "detail" and "lang".  Sending an envelope
   to a third party discloses its content, so local verification is
   RECOMMENDED.  A verification service MUST NOT return commercial
   status, duplicate information, or account numbers except to the
   issuer, the recipient, or parties they authorize.

12.  Operational Considerations

   Signing keys of participants and nodes need non-exportable storage,
   such as hardware security modules or equivalent cloud services.
   Operators need a procedure to tell participants and counterparties
   about a key compromise.

   Networks and jurisdictions set their own requirements on platforms,
   for example certification to ISO/IEC 27001.  A node that also acts in
   a designated channel holds whatever accreditation that channel
   requires, and trust lists can record such accreditations.

   Retention periods for invoices and accounting records are set by each
   jurisdiction and can span decades.  Recipients SHOULD store, with
   each envelope, the Participant Document, attestations, status list
   snapshots, trust lists, the keys and latest checkpoints of the
   transparency services, and Receipts used at verification, so that the
   verification can be repeated after the issuer's domain or operator
   has changed or ceased.  Long-term validity of signatures can be
   preserved with evidence records [RFC4998], with JAdES archival
   signatures [JADES], or by a preservation service, for example one
   that follows [ETSI-PRES].  Signatures inside the legal original
   ("native_signature") are validated, and validated again later, with
   the procedures of their own format, for example [ETSI-VAL] for AdES
   signatures.  The transparency salt is kept as long as the envelope;
   deleting it breaks the link between the envelope and its commitment.

   Some jurisdictions require that invoices be stored in their
   territory, or that the place of storage be notified or accessible
   online to the tax authority.  Nodes describe their storage in the
   "storage" member of their Node Document, and a node that cannot meet
   the requirements that apply to a recipient answers
   "storageJurisdiction".

   Network access across borders is not uniform.  In some jurisdictions
   connections to hosts abroad can be slow, filtered, or blocked, public
   web sites need a local filing, and DNSSEC validation is uncommon.
   Participants there are expected to use nodes, transparency services,
   status list endpoints, and trust list distribution points that are
   reachable from within the jurisdiction, and a sending node abroad can
   deliver through a node in the recipient's jurisdiction that the
   recipient lists in "inbound".

   When a participant changes operator, the former operator publishes a
   last document with "superseded_by", and keeps serving its last
   Participant Document for at least the pin period it announced.  When
   a participant changes PID through a merger or re-registration, the
   successor lists the former PID in "predecessors".  Envelopes issued
   under the former PID remain verifiable with the former keys.

   Pinning (Section 14.3) restricts the use of payment details that
   arrive through unlisted channels.  It never prevents an invoice from
   being issued through a channel that the law requires, so an outage of
   a participant's node does not stop invoicing within legal deadlines.

   Participants with low bandwidth or intermittent connectivity, and
   fiscal devices that work offline, usually rely on a node hosted by
   their operator, which queues envelopes and registrations and retries
   as described in Section 8.4.  Nodes cache Participant Documents,
   trust lists, and status lists within their validity, and senders
   avoid "href" documents and large attachments for recipients that
   announce a small "max_envelope_size".

   Transparency services and witnesses have to run for as long as
   invoices are retained.  Operators of nodes are expected to choose
   services with published policies on availability and retention, and
   to register with at least one service that is independent of
   themselves.

   Sanctions, controls on cryptography, and data localization rules can
   prevent a participant in one jurisdiction from using a transparency
   service, witness, trust list, or attestation issuer operated in
   another, or can end that use while invoices are retained.  ITP names
   no global operator: relying parties configure these services per
   jurisdiction, an envelope can carry Receipts from several
   transparency services, and verification uses the evidence that the
   recipient stored with the envelope.

   For manageability ([I-D.ietf-opsawg-rfc5706bis]), nodes are expected
   to log, for each envelope, the result of each verification step and
   the evidence used, to count deliveries, rejections by error type, and
   verification failures by step, and to warn their operators before
   trust lists, attestations, Participant Documents, or node keys
   expire.  These counts are the data that the experiment asks
   recipients to publish.

14.  Security Considerations

14.1.  Threat Model

   The attacker can read, modify, and inject network traffic; control a
   supplier's or a buyer's email account; register look-alike domains
   and shell companies; run a malicious directory or control an
   authoritative DNS server; compromise a participant's key, an
   operator, or an employee of an attestation issuer; and place text in
   invoice content that software processing it may act on.  ITP aims to
   let a recipient detect forged or altered invoices and changed payment
   details, and to let participants detect keys and attestations issued
   without their consent.  It does not protect against a participant
   that issues false invoices with its own valid key, including
   unsolicited invoices to parties it has never dealt with, against a
   buyer that gives another party's tax identifier, or against
   compromise of the recipient's own systems (Section 14.4).

14.2.  Impersonation and Trust

   Signatures identify a legal entity only through the trust model of
   Section 6, and their assurance is limited by that of the attestation
   issuers in the trust lists that a relying party accepts.  A relying
   party that accepts an inadequately vetted trust list, or obtains one
   over an unauthenticated channel, accepts any key its issuers attest.
   Trust lists are signed and expire, and relying parties are expected
   to obtain the signing keys of the lists they use out of band, as they
   do for other trust anchors.  Restricting each issuer to its
   jurisdictions limits the damage a single issuer can do.

   ITP does not establish that an issuer is a genuine supplier of the
   recipient.  It addresses one kind of supplier fraud: an envelope that
   claims to come from a supplier the recipient knows, or that changes
   that supplier's payment details, fails verification unless an
   attestation issuer that the recipient accepts has bound the sender's
   key to that supplier's PID.  It does not address an invoice from a
   legal entity that exists but has never supplied the recipient,
   possibly with the help of an insider: that envelope verifies.  The
   receiving node reports it as coming from a new issuer (Section 11),
   and the recipient can check the "kyb" attestations of the issuer,
   where present (Section 6.3).  Recipients detect such invoices with
   their own controls, such as supplier onboarding and matching invoices
   against orders and receipts.

   Envelopes from look-alike domains, re-registered expired domains, and
   shell companies with genuine registrations verify.  The damage is
   limited because "domain-control" attestations cover the legal entity
   and the key and expire after 90 days; a document accepted under rule
   2 cannot replace one accepted under rules 1 or 3; recently bound keys
   are flagged for 72 hours; and verification does not authorize
   payment.  Recipients still need to match the issuer's PID against
   their own supplier records.

   A compromised or dishonest attestation issuer can bind an attacker's
   key to a victim's PID.  The "issuers" field of the DNS record limits
   which honest issuers will do so, although without DNSSEC an attacker
   can strip it from the responses an issuer sees.  Registration of
   attestations under the PID digest lets the victim's monitor detect a
   mis-issued attestation, and the 72-hour rules in Section 6.2 give a
   participant that watches the logs, itself or through a monitor, time
   to react before payment details change.  A participant that does not
   watch them learns of a mis-issued attestation only from its
   counterparties.

14.3.  Discovery and Downgrade

   Without DNSSEC, an attacker that controls DNS responses can remove
   the "_itp" record or serve a different well-known resource.  That
   alone does not let it impersonate a participant, because acceptance
   requires the rules of Section 6.2, and rule 2 requires DNSSEC.  It
   can, however, make a participant look absent and push a sender to
   another channel.  Pinning is carried in the signed Participant
   Document, not in DNS or in the unsigned well-known resource, so an
   attacker who controls only those cannot set or extend it.  Clients
   that have accepted a participant's document keep it for
   "pin_max_age", and payment details that arrive through other channels
   during that time are not used automatically.  As with MTA-STS
   (Section 10.2 of [RFC8461]), pinning does not protect the first
   contact.

14.4.  First Contact

   A typical first contact starts when a buyer gives a seller its tax
   identifier, for example at checkout.  The seller's node normalizes
   the identifier to a PID (Section 4) and SHOULD check its format and
   check digit and, where the tax authority offers a validation service,
   that it is registered.  It obtains a Participant Document for the PID
   from a directory that it is configured to use or that a profile
   designates, or from the "_itp" record and well-known resource of a
   domain that the buyer gave, and accepts it only under Section 6.2.
   For a buyer without a domain this means rule 1 or rule 3, which do
   not depend on DNS.  If no document is accepted, the seller uses
   another channel.  The seller's node verifies the Node Document of the
   "itp" inbound entry with a key listed in "kids" of that entry and
   delivers (Section 8.3).  The receiving node obtains the seller's
   Participant Document from "issuer_participant_document" or from a
   directory, accepts it under the same rules, and checks that the
   sending node is listed in "senders".

   A party that controls DNS, a directory, or the network cannot direct
   the envelope to a node of its choosing, or have its own envelope
   accepted in the name of a participant, unless an attestation issuer
   or certification authority that the relying party accepts binds its
   key to that participant's PID.  It can make a participant look
   absent, so that the seller uses another channel, and it can serve an
   older document that is still validly signed; attestations that cover
   compromised keys are revoked (Section 6.3), so such a document is
   then no longer accepted.

   At first contact ITP does not protect against the following.  It does
   not check that the buyer that gave the tax identifier is entitled to
   use it: the envelope is delivered to the holder of the PID, so a
   wrong or misused identifier discloses the invoice to a third party
   and gives that party an invoice it did not order.  Pinning does not
   apply, because no document has been accepted before.  A key that an
   attestation issuer binds to a PID by mistake or fraud is flagged as
   recently bound for 72 hours (Section 6.2) and is then accepted like
   any other key, so this rule protects only participants that watch
   transparency services, themselves or through a monitor (Section 6.5).
   Any legal entity with an accepted attestation, including a shell
   company, can deliver envelopes to a participant whose PID it knows;
   there is no step in which the recipient consents to a new sender.
   Such envelopes verify, are reported as coming from a new issuer, and
   do not authorize payment (Section 11).  Recipients match them against
   their own orders and supplier records, and payment details in them
   are handled as described in [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle].

14.5.  Tampering, Type Confusion, and Algorithm Confusion

   The envelope core, including the document digest, the list of
   signature algorithms, and payment details, is covered by the issuer
   signature.  Every signed object defined by ITP has a distinct "typ",
   and verifiers check it, so a signature made for one kind of object
   cannot be presented as another [RFC8725].  "itp_role" is listed in
   "crit", so a generic JOSE library cannot silently ignore it.  Each
   key carries "alg", and verifiers do not take the algorithm from the
   message.  Keys embedded in headers are used only when they match an
   accepted "kid".  Extra signatures that fail are ignored, so they
   cannot be used to invalidate a valid envelope, and "sig_algs" makes
   the removal of a signature detectable.

14.6.  Parser Differentials and Canonicalization

   Two JSON parsers can read the same text differently, for example when
   member names are duplicated, and this can make a signer and a
   verifier see different values.  ITP requires I-JSON and requires
   rejection of duplicate names and unpaired surrogates before any other
   processing.  Verification is done over the octets received, so
   differences between JCS implementations affect only the signer's
   ability to reproduce its own payload.  Non-integer numbers are
   excluded because their serialization is where JCS libraries differ
   most.  Documents are carried as octets.  XML documents bring their
   own risks, such as external entities and signature wrapping when
   XAdES is used; parsers are configured as required in Section 7.2, and
   signatures inside documents are verified with the rules of their own
   format.

14.7.  Delegated Keys and Misbehaving Operators

   An operator that holds delegated signing keys can sign envelopes for
   its participants.  It cannot change the participant's keys, senders,
   archive node, or pin, because Participant Documents are signed only
   with keys the participant holds.  New keys need the old key or a
   logged attestation, revocation is monotonic, and participants or
   their monitors can watch transparency services.  Participants that
   need stronger guarantees keep their own keys, for example in
   qualified signature creation devices.

14.8.  Status Forgery and Divergent State

   Statuses can have legal and tax consequences, and several
   jurisdictions report them to the tax administration.  Without further
   rules, a receiving operator, or an attacker that compromises it,
   could sign them in a participant's name.  Section 9 therefore
   requires the signature of the party named in "by", and nodes can sign
   only "delivered" and "technical-rejected".  Both nodes keep and
   exchange the complete set of messages, and
   [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle] derives the state from that set, so that
   the two sides cannot end up with different states because messages
   arrived in a different order.

14.9.  Evidence Stripping and Substitution

   "itp_evidence" is not signed by the issuer.  An intermediary can
   remove a stamp or a Receipt, or add a rendition.  "required_evidence"
   lets verifiers notice removal, renditions are accepted only from
   bridges accepted for the scheme, and conflicting renditions are
   reported to the user (see Section 7.3).

14.10.  Replay and Duplicates

   Requests carry server-issued nonces and short validity periods.
   Envelopes and status messages carry identifiers, status messages are
   chained with "seq" and "prev", and receiving nodes detect duplicate
   envelopes by digest, also when they arrive from two authorized nodes
   or after the idempotency window (Section 8.4).

14.11.  Copies for the Issuer

   Copies go only to the node named in "archive" of the issuer's
   accepted Participant Document, which only a non-delegated key of the
   participant can change (Section 5.5).  Content of an envelope, a
   directory, or DNS cannot direct copies to another node.  The "copies"
   endpoint is covered by the HTTP Message Signature, so an intermediary
   cannot turn a delivery into a copy, which would leave the recipient
   without the envelope, or a copy into a delivery.  The archive node
   signs no status message about a copy and sends nothing to other
   nodes, so a copy is never counted as delivery, and the counterparty
   receives nothing from the archive node and is not told that a copy
   exists.  A participant that names an archive node run by another
   operator gives that operator the content of every document issued in
   its name; the "storage" member of that node's Node Document
   (Section 5.6) says where it keeps them.

14.12.  Key Compromise and Back-Dating

   An attacker with a stolen key can sign envelopes with any stated
   date.  Validity is therefore judged against registration time in a
   transparency service or a trusted timestamp, and revocation records
   the time of compromise.  Envelopes signed with a compromised key and
   lacking earlier trusted time evidence are rejected.  Some risk
   remains for the interval between compromise and its detection.

14.13.  Log Equivocation

   A transparency service could show different logs to different
   parties.  Witness cosigning and checked consistency proofs address
   this, as long as the witnesses do not collude with the log.
   Verifiers that only check Receipts depend on the honesty of the
   transparency service.

14.14.  Server-Side Request Forgery

   ITP objects contain URLs that a node retrieves.  The rules in
   Section 8 restrict them to HTTPS, to the expected origins for
   documents, and to public addresses checked at connection time, which
   also covers DNS rebinding.

14.15.  Untrusted Content

   Line descriptions, reason texts, and attachments are written by the
   counterparty.  Software that acts on invoices, including software
   agents, needs to keep such text apart from its own instructions;
   [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates] discusses this for agents.

   A document can carry an image for people besides its structured data,
   as in hybrid PDF and XML formats, and attachments can include a PDF
   of the invoice.  The two can differ, for example in the payment
   account shown; in Germany, for example, the structured part prevails.
   Software that shows invoices to people is expected to show a
   rendering generated from the structured data of the verified
   document, or to mark an embedded image as not checked against it.

14.16.  Denial of Service

   Nodes check size, nesting depth, and the number of signatures before
   canonicalization or cryptographic operations, verify the HTTP Message
   Signature before any signature check on the envelope (Section 8.3),
   limit the retrievals caused by one request, and rate-limit by sending
   node.  Envelopes whose issuer has no accepted Participant Document
   are refused with "untrustedIssuer" (Section 11).  Where the law that
   applies to the recipient allows it to refuse invoices delivered
   through ITP, a receiving node MAY also refuse, with the same error,
   envelopes from issuers that do not meet a policy set by the
   recipient, for example issuers it has not listed or that hold no
   attestation of a given type.

14.17.  Algorithm Lifetime

   Invoices and accounting records are retained for periods that can
   outlast the security of current signature algorithms if a large
   quantum computer is built.  ITP allows additional signatures with
   other algorithms on the same envelope, and "sig_algs" prevents those
   signatures from being stripped.  Evidence records can renew
   protection of archived envelopes.  A later revision is expected to
   say when envelopes with only classical signatures stop being accepted
   for new issuance.

15.  Privacy Considerations

   This section follows [RFC6973].

   Data:  Invoices contain commercially sensitive data and, when a party
      is a natural person, personal data.  The fact that two parties
      exchange invoices is sensitive too.

   Public and non-public data:  Anyone can obtain, without
      authentication: the "_itp" record and well-known resource of a
      domain; any Participant Document whose URL or PID it knows, with
      the participant's PIDs, name, keys, attestations, and the URLs of
      the nodes that receive, send, and keep copies for it, which
      usually identify its operator; Node Documents, which do not list
      participants; and the registrations in transparency services
      described below, which do not identify recipients.  A party that
      knows or guesses a PID can therefore learn whether that
      participant uses ITP and through which operator.  ITP does not
      publish envelopes, documents, attachments, status messages,
      payment details or "payment-account" attestations, an operator's
      list of participants, or a participant's counterparties.  Nodes
      return envelopes and status messages only to the nodes of the
      parties (Section 5.6), and the inbox does not reveal whether a PID
      is a participant to clients that fail authentication
      (Section 8.3).

   Natural persons:  A sole trader's tax identifier is often a national
      identity number, is derived from one, or encodes the holder's name
      and date of birth (an Indonesian resident's NPWP is the national
      identity number, and an Indian GSTIN contains the holder's PAN),
      so publishing it discloses personal data.  Participants that are
      natural persons SHOULD NOT publish a PID in DNS, SHOULD omit
      "name", and SHOULD be reachable only through exact-match queries.
      PIDs do not appear in URLs (Section 5.6), so they do not reach
      access logs.

   Enumeration:  Tax identifier spaces are small enough to enumerate.
      Directories answer only exact queries and rate-limit them
      (Section 5.7), which slows enumeration by one client but does not
      prevent it by many; a directory that answers only authenticated
      operators makes bulk queries attributable.  PID digests of legal
      entities are unkeyed hashes, so a reader of a transparency log can
      test every identifier of a jurisdiction and learn which legal
      entities use ITP and when their documents or attestations changed.
      ITP therefore does not keep confidential which legal entities use
      it, and an operator's customer list can be rebuilt by querying
      directories for those PIDs.  PID digests of natural persons are
      keyed (Section 10) to prevent this for them.

   Observers:  Resolvers see "_itp" queries, and directories and
      verification services see who looks up whom.  Clients SHOULD fetch
      status lists whole and cache them, and SHOULD verify locally.
      They can also use encrypted DNS transports [RFC7858] [RFC8484] and
      Oblivious HTTP [RFC9458] for third-party directories and
      verification services.  Retrieving a document from "href" tells
      its host when the recipient processed it, which is one reason
      "content_b64" is RECOMMENDED.

   Transparency:  In per-envelope mode the key thumbprint in "iss" links
      each registration to the issuer's published keys, so readers of
      the log learn how many envelopes each issuer registers and when;
      this can reveal sales volume.  In batch mode they learn only per-
      node volumes.  Registrations of Participant Documents and
      attestations reveal that a given legal entity uses ITP.  Signed
      Statements MUST NOT contain identifiers of natural persons in
      clear.

   Identifiers and correlation:  PIDs are stable public identifiers of
      legal entities and ITP does not hide them.  UUIDv7 identifiers
      reveal their creation time.

   Confidentiality:  Nodes SHOULD encrypt stored envelopes.  Envelopes
      MAY also be encrypted end to end to a recipient key with JWE
      [RFC7516]; a bridge that needs the content then acts on behalf of
      the party that uses it.

   Retention and erasure:  Invoices are kept for the periods the law
      requires.  Data protection laws commonly allow such retention, for
      example Article 17(3) of [GDPR] in the European Union.  Many of
      them also restrict transfers of personal data abroad or require a
      copy in the country.  Registering in a transparency service,
      querying a directory, or using a node, bridge, or verification
      service in another country can be such a transfer when a party is
      a natural person.  Operators state where they process data, as
      nodes do in "storage" (Section 5.6).  Logs are append-only, so
      data that could identify a natural person is not registered in
      them, as recommended, for example, in [EDPB-BC].  Each component
      (node, directory, transparency service, verifier, attestation
      issuer, bridge) needs a documented role under the data protection
      law that applies to it.

16.  Internationalization Considerations

   This section follows [RFC2277] and [RFC6365].

   All ITP JSON texts are UTF-8.  Members that hold text for people
   (such as "name" in a Participant Document, "reason.text" in a status
   message, and "detail" in problem details and verification results)
   are not used for matching.  Their language is given by a "lang"
   member, a language tag [RFC5646], in the same object, and their base
   direction by an optional "dir" member ("ltr", "rtl", or "auto");
   without "lang", the language is unknown.  A sender MAY provide the
   same text in several languages by using an array of objects, each
   with "lang", "text", and optional "dir", in place of a string, where
   a member's definition allows it.  Some jurisdictions require names
   and other text in a given language and allow a second one, for
   example Arabic with English.  Receivers display such text with its
   language and direction, each value in its own bidirectional isolate
   [UAX9] (for example between U+2068 FIRST STRONG ISOLATE and U+2069
   POP DIRECTIONAL ISOLATE), so that right-to-left text does not reorder
   neighboring identifiers or amounts, and do not interpret it.  Text
   that nodes generate, such as "detail" in problem details, follows the
   Accept-Language field of the request when possible (Section 8.8).

   Identifiers that are matched (PIDs, invoice numbers, URLs, codes) are
   restricted to ASCII or percent-encoded UTF-8 (Section 4), or are NFC
   strings under the PRECIS FreeformClass (Section 2.3), so that
   matching does not depend on how text was entered.

   Documents carry their own language information, and
   "document_languages" in the core lets a recipient know, without
   parsing the document, in which languages it is written, for example
   where a jurisdiction requires a given language.

   Calendar dates are Gregorian "full-date" values.  Where a document
   uses another calendar, the core still carries the Gregorian date, and
   the document keeps its original date.

   except where noted:

   Type name:  application

   Required parameters:  N/A

   Optional parameters:  N/A

   Encoding considerations:  binary.  The "+jws" types use the JWS JSON
      Serialization, which is UTF-8 JSON; "itp-attestation+sd-jwt" uses
      the SD-JWT serialization; "itp-metadata+json" is UTF-8 JSON; "itp-
      commitment" and "itp-batch-head" are 32 octets.

      JSON.  They contain no active content.  Integrity and origin come
      from the signatures, which are checked as described in Section 6
      and Section 11 of this document; the content is otherwise
      untrusted.  Envelopes and status messages can contain commercially
      sensitive and personal data and are not to be cached by shared
      caches.  "itp-metadata+json" is unsigned and carries no trust on
      its own.  "itp-commitment" reveals nothing about the envelope
      without its salt.  See the Security and Privacy Considerations
      sections of this document.

   Instructions to the designated experts:

   *  A new value must not duplicate an existing one, and its meaning
      must be defined precisely in a publicly available specification.

   *  A PID scheme or tax identifier type must name the authority that
      issues the identifiers, give a normalization rule that makes
      comparison unambiguous, and point to a public description of the
      format and check digits.  Types whose identifiers are commonly
      derived from national identity numbers need a note on privacy.

   *  A bridge scheme must name the jurisdiction or network, the
      authority or organization that runs it, and the public
      specification of its interface.

   *  A status must say who emits it, whether it is final, and how it
      maps to the statuses of existing networks where such a mapping
      exists.

   *  A signer role or attestation type affects security.  Its
      specification must say what it may and may not substitute for and
      what a relying party checks before accepting it, and must have
      Security Considerations.  Experts are encouraged to seek review on
      the mailing list where this document is discussed.

   *  Experts are encouraged to consult the organizations responsible
      for the network or jurisdiction concerned, such as OpenPeppol for
      Peppol identifiers, the operators of other networks for their
      identifiers, and the tax administration for national codes.

   *  Identifier formats change.  Experts can approve updates to an
      existing PID scheme or tax identifier type, such as a new format
      or prefix, and keep the earlier rules in the entry while
      identifiers issued under them can still appear in retained
      envelopes.

   For every entry: the issuing authority is the tax administration of
   the jurisdiction; the format and check-digit reference is that
   administration's published specification; the normalization is the
   default of Section 4 plus the prefix and suffix rules in the table;
   "personal identifier" is "yes" for entries marked "P" and "no"
   otherwise; and the reference is this document.

   Some entries need more detail.  "CN uscc" is the unified social
   credit code, which registration authorities assign to legal persons
   and other organizations; it replaced the codes that departments,
   including the tax administration, assigned separately.  An "SG gst"
   number is either the UEN, which the agency that registered the entity
   assigns, or a number that IRAS assigns, for example to a GST group,
   whose members then share it.  The "NZ gst" number is the IRD number;
   an IRD number of 8 digits is written with a leading "0".  "NG tin" is
   the Tax ID that the Nigeria Revenue Service and the Joint Revenue
   Board issue jointly, which replaced earlier tax identification
   numbers from 1 January 2026.  In DIAN's electronic invoice the NIT is
   written without its check digit, which is carried in a separate
   attribute; the "CO nit" PID includes the check digit.  For natural
   persons, "CO nit", "CL rut", "PE ruc", "AR cuit", "TR vkn", "IL tin",
   "VN mst", and "ID npwp" are or contain a national identity number.
   "CA gst-hst", "VN mst-unit", and "ID nitku" are derived from the base
   identifiers "CA bn", "VN mst", and "ID npwp".

   The table has more entries for the member states of the European
   Union because their VAT identification numbers share one structure
   and one validation service, which made them easy to check.  It sets
   no priority among jurisdictions, and entries for any jurisdiction are
   registered on the same terms (Section 17.7).

   The jurisdiction of a "tax" PID is the one whose register the
   identifier belongs to, written with its [ISO3166] code: a Greek VAT
   number written "EL000000000" in a document is "tax:GR:vat:000000000".
   VAT numbers that the Isle of Man issues in the register of the United
   Kingdom use "GB", and those that Monaco issues in the register of
   France use "FR".

   For the member states of the European Union, the "vat" type is the
   VAT identification number assigned under Article 214 of Directive
   2006/112/EC, written without its prefix.  Whether the number is valid
   in [VIES] at a given time is checked by a "tax-registration"
   attestation and does not change the PID.  Where the national tax
   identifier has the same value as the VAT identification number
   without its prefix, as in Spain or Portugal, the "vat" type is used
   for both, so that one entity has one PID.  National identifiers with
   another value, such as the German Steuernummer or the French SIREN,
   have types of their own.  "XI" identifies traders in Northern Ireland
   for supplies of goods under the Windsor Framework, with the same
   number as "GB".  "XI" numbers are validated by [VIES]; "GB" numbers
   are not.  Both spellings map to the same PID, so the PID does not say
   which prefix a document requires; software that writes a document
   from a PID takes the prefix from the transaction, and a "tax-
   registration" attestation for a trader in Northern Ireland records
   the "XI" check in "check".

   Identification numbers of the special schemes of the European Union
   for distance sales are issued by the member state of identification,
   whose [ISO3166] numeric code they contain: "EU" and 9 characters
   under the non-Union scheme and "IM" and 10 characters under the
   import scheme.  They are "tax" PIDs with that member state as
   jurisdiction and the types "oss" and "ioss", with the prefix kept in
   the identifier, for example "tax:IE:ioss:IM3720000000".  "EU" is not
   a jurisdiction code.  The Union scheme uses the "vat" PID of the
   trader.  [VIES] does not validate these numbers.

C.7.  Negative Test Cases

   Implementations are expected to produce the following results.  Each
   case starts from the first envelope of Appendix C and changes one
   thing.

   1.  A second signature with a random 64-octet value and a valid
       protected header is appended to "signatures".  The envelope is
       valid; the extra signature is reported as failed and ignored
       (Section 7.5).

   2.  The protected header of the issuer signature omits "crit".  The
       signature is rejected, and the envelope is invalid.

   3.  The core lists "sig_algs" with the values "Ed25519" and "ML-DSA-
       65", only the Ed25519 signature is present, and the verifier
       supports ML-DSA-65.  The envelope is invalid.  A verifier that
       does not support ML-DSA-65 accepts it.

   4.  The core contains "total_with_tax": "300.000" with "currency":
       "EUR".  The envelope is invalid, because EUR has two decimals.
       "1000" with "JPY" and "1.000" with "KWD" are valid; "-0" and
       "-0.00" are invalid in any currency.

   5.  The core contains the member "total_with_tax" twice.  The
       envelope is rejected before any signature check.

   6.  The issuer signature has "itp_role" "issuer" but its key is
       listed in "delegates" of the Participant Document.  The signature
       is rejected.

   7.  A Participant Document with a greater "seq" omits a key that the
       accepted document contained.  The new document is rejected.

   8.  The same envelope is delivered twice with different
       "itp_evidence" contents.  The second delivery receives the
       original "delivered" status, and the new evidence is added to the
       stored envelope.

Appendix D.  Design Rationale

D.1.  JOSE for Envelopes, COSE for Transparency

   Invoice software is built around JSON and HTTP, so envelopes use
   JOSE.  Transparency services standardized by SCITT use COSE, so
   commitments are registered as COSE Signed Statements.  JCS is used so
   that the signer can reproduce the envelope core for audit; verifiers
   do not depend on it, because they verify the octets received, and
   documents are carried as octets.  JCS is an Informational RFC, so a
   Standards Track successor of this document would need a downward
   reference or a different serialization.

D.2.  Registered Media Types for Signed Objects

   Explicit typing prevents cross-object substitution [RFC8725].  The
   "typ" values are registered as media types with the "+jws" structured
   syntax suffix, as listed in the IANA "Structured Syntax Suffixes"
   registry with reference to [RFC7515], so that each value has a single
   owner and meaning.

D.3.  TXT Record

   [RFC5507] discourages overloading TXT records.  ITP follows the
   precedent of MTA-STS [RFC8461]: the record signals presence and a
   version, plus a key thumbprint that is useful only with DNSSEC, and
   all other data is retrieved over HTTPS.  An SVCB record [RFC9460] was
   considered and not used, because no connection parameters need to be
   conveyed.

D.4.  An Opaque Inbox

   Earlier designs put the recipient's PID in the inbox URL.  The
   recipient is already in the signed core, and a PID in a URL ends up
   in access logs and can be altered by frameworks that decode percent-
   encoding, which would break HTTP Message Signatures.  The inbox is
   therefore one URL per node.

D.5.  No Invoice Model of Its Own

   ITP does not define an invoice model, and does not make one
   mandatory.  Tax authorities prescribe the content and often the
   syntax of the legal original, and they change them more often than an
   Internet protocol can follow; a model of its own would compete with
   EN 16931, Peppol PINT, and the national formats instead of carrying
   them.  The envelope core holds only the members that transport,
   duplicate detection, and verification need, the way an email header
   does for its body.  UBL is the one syntax that every receiving node
   accepts, because it is the common base of Peppol, PINT, and several
   national formats.  Canonical models such as GOBL [GOBL] are useful
   for converting between formats and can be carried as documents or
   renditions, but a model maintained by one organization is not made
   mandatory.

D.6.  One Scheme for Tax Identifiers

   A separate scheme for VAT identification numbers of the European
   Union was considered and not adopted, because it would treat one
   region as the default.  All tax identifiers use the "tax" scheme with
   the [ISO3166] code of the jurisdiction that issued them; prefixes
   such as "EL" or "XI" are presentation details recorded in the
   registry.

D.7.  Generic Fiscal Members

   Tax regimes differ in what they require: identifiers assigned by the
   authority, signatures inside the document, hash chains, fiscal
   devices, contingency modes, several taxes on one invoice, currencies
   with zero or three decimals.  The core carries these as generic
   members ("fiscal_codes", "native_signature", "chain",
   "fiscal_device", "issuance_mode", "tax_summary") backed by
   registries, and leaves the rules of each regime to profiles.  As a
   result, no normative requirement names a country; examples do, and
   identifiers, codes, and rules specific to a jurisdiction are registry
   entries and profiles.

D.8.  Why Not BDXL

   BDXL [BDXL] resolves a hashed participant identifier in a DNS zone
   run by the network, which then points to an SMP.  This suits a
   network with a central register of participants.  ITP discovery is
   controlled by the participant from its own domain, without a central
   register, so it uses a record under that domain.  The two can
   coexist: a Participant Document can list a Peppol endpoint, and a
   BDXL zone could list ITP endpoints.

D.9.  Why Not Signed Email

   Invoices sent by email can be signed with S/MIME [RFC8551], and DKIM
   [RFC6376] lets a receiving mail system check the domain that sent a
   message.  A DKIM signature is checked when the message is received
   and binds the message to a sending domain, not to the legal entity
   named in the invoice.  S/MIME certificates identify a mailbox and, in
   some certificate profiles, an organization, and their discovery
   through DNS [RFC8162] starts from an email address.  Neither finds a
   receiving endpoint from a tax identifier, binds a payment account, or
   defines statuses.  An ITP envelope can be sent as an email attachment
   and verified like any other (Section 11).

D.10.  Why Not Only an Existing Network

   Four-corner networks such as Peppol and the Digital Business Networks
   Alliance [DBNA], exchange platforms that tax authorities run or
   designate, and EDI value-added networks provide addressing and
   transport among their admitted members.  ITP adds verification that
   does not depend on the delivering channel, transparency, and
   addressing for entities outside those networks.  ITP envelopes can
   travel over them, and the identifiers of a network that registers a
   PID scheme, such as Peppol identifiers, are ITP PIDs.

   An issuer-signed payload that can be verified independently of the
   channel may be useful inside such a network as well.  Carrying the
   envelope defined here as a payload of a Peppol business document
   would need a document type and process identifier, and rules for
   access points that convert documents (Section 7.4), that OpenPeppol
   would have to define; this document does not define them.

   In clearance regimes, the legal original often already carries a
   signature or seal of the issuer and a stamp of the tax authority that
   any holder can check, for example through a public verification
   service of the authority.  ITP carries those artifacts as renditions
   and stamps (Section 7.6) and verifies them under the rules of their
   scheme.  It adds discovery, the binding of keys to legal entities
   across jurisdictions, attested payment accounts
   ([I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle]), and one verification procedure for
   invoices from different regimes.

Appendix E.  Open Issues

   1.  Stream.  The registrations in Section 17 assume the IETF stream.
       On the Independent Submission stream, the URN sub-namespace and
       the standards-tree media types would have to change.

   2.  Mandatory syntax.  This document requires receiving nodes to
       accept UBL 2.1 or later.  Several regimes use other syntaxes,
       such as CFDI in Mexico, NF-e and NFS-e in Brazil, DTE in Chile,
       FatturaPA in Italy, the JSON schema INV-01 in India, and national
       XML schemas in China, South Korea, Taiwan, Viet Nam, and
       Indonesia, and invoicing between large trading partners in the
       United States relies mostly on ASC X12 810.  Malaysia uses UBL
       2.1 in XML or JSON, so whether the UBL requirement covers the
       JSON syntax of UBL is also open.  Options are a second mandatory
       syntax, or a rule that a receiving node accepts the legal-
       original syntax of each registered profile for a jurisdiction in
       which its participant holds a "tax" PID.  This document takes the
       second option as a SHOULD (Section 3.3).

   3.  Initial entries of the ITP Tax Identifier Types, Tax Schemes, and
       Bridge Schemes registries, which need input from the authorities
       concerned.

   4.  Alignment of "kyb" attestations with SD-JWT VC types, verifiable
       credentials [W3C-VC], and the European Business Wallet [EU-BW].

   5.  Relationship with electronic transferable records
       [UNCITRAL-MLETR] for invoices that are traded as receivables.

   6.  Whether the transparency profile (Section 10) should become a
       separate document for the SCITT working group.

   7.  Governance and funding of shared transparency services,
       witnesses, and monitors.

   8.  Event notifications to software that consumes a node, which this
       version leaves out.

   9.  Whether the envelope should be profiled for use inside Peppol and
       similar networks (Appendix D.10), which is for those networks to
       decide.
