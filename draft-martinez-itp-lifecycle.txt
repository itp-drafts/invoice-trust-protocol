 Invoice Trust Protocol (ITP): Lifecycle Status and Payment Information
                  draft-martinez-itp-lifecycle-latest

Abstract

   This document extends the core of the Invoice Trust Protocol (ITP)
   with commercial, tax, and payment statuses for invoices, a
   deterministic way for both parties to derive the same state from the
   status messages they exchange, payment information for the envelope
   core, and attestations for payment accounts.  Informative appendices
   relate ITP statuses to those of Peppol and of several national
   systems.

1.  Introduction

   The core of the Invoice Trust Protocol (ITP) [I-D.martinez-itp-core]
   delivers a signed invoice and returns a signed "delivered" status.
   After delivery, the buyer may accept or reject the invoice, the tax
   authority may need to be informed, the receivable may be assigned to
   a factor, and the invoice is paid, possibly in installments or after
   a credit note.  Several jurisdictions now require some of these
   events to be reported, with deadlines, and give legal effect to the
   absence of a response.

   This document defines:

   *  statuses for those events and who may sign each (Section 3);

   *  how a node derives the state of an envelope from the set of status
      messages, so that both parties reach the same result regardless of
      the order in which messages arrived (Section 4);

   *  hooks for jurisdiction rules on deadlines and cancellation
      (Section 5);

   *  the "payment" member of the envelope core (Section 6); and

   *  an attestation type that binds a payment account to its holder
      (Section 7).

   The meaning of commercial statuses is already defined elsewhere, for
   example by tax authorities and by networks such as Peppol, whose
   Invoice Response [PEPPOL-IR] defines them.  ITP defines comparable
   statuses, carries them in signed messages, and relates them to those
   of several systems in Appendix B.  Software agents can sign some of
   these statuses within the limits of a mandate
   [I-D.martinez-itp-agent-mandates].

1.1.  The Experiment

   This document is Experimental.  The experiment is whether two
   independent implementations derive the same state from the same set
   of messages, including messages that arrive in different orders, and
   whether the hooks in Section 5 are enough to express the rules of at
   least three jurisdictions with different models.

   Results will be reported on the mailing list where this document is
   discussed and in later revisions of it.  The experiment ends 24
   months after publication, together with that of
   [I-D.martinez-itp-core].

2.  Conventions and Definitions

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

   Terms and conventions of [I-D.martinez-itp-core] apply, including
   those for amounts, text, and language tags.  In addition:

   Payee:  The party to which payment is due: the seller, a party named
      as payee in the invoice, or the assignee after an assignment of
      the receivable.

3.  Statuses

3.1.  Additional Members of Status Messages

   Status messages have the format defined in [I-D.martinez-itp-core].
   For the statuses in this document they can also contain:

   amount, currency:  For partial acceptance, payment, credit, or
      failure.  "currency" is an [ISO4217] code and defaults to the
      invoice currency.

   settled_amount, exchange_rate:  For a payment or a failure in a
      currency other than the invoice currency: the amount in the
      invoice currency that it settles, as stated by its signer, and the
      rate applied, in the form of "exchange_rate" in the envelope core,
      with "rate" giving the number of units of the invoice currency per
      unit of "currency".

   conditions (string), lang:  Text of conditions attached to an
      acceptance.

   refers_to (string):  The "id" of an earlier status message that this
      one answers, such as the "payment-sent" that a "payment-failed"
      concerns.

   corrective (string):  For "credited", REQUIRED: the "id" of the
      envelope of the corrective document.  For each corrective
      document, only the counted "credited" with the lowest "id" is
      summed in "amount_credited".

   deadline (object):  For "deemed-accepted", "deemed-rejected", and
      similar statuses: "from" (the "id" of the status message the
      period starts from or, for a stamp, which has no "id", the
      base64url SHA-256 digest of the stamp's JWS Payload), "days" or
      "hours", "calendar" (a value from the ITP Calendars registry, such
      as "calendar-days" or a "business-days-XX" calendar of Section 5),
      and "until" (the date, or for periods in hours the "date-time",
      computed under the profile).

   effective_at (string):  The "date-time" of the event, in UTC as are
      all ITP timestamps ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]), for statuses whose
      period the profile counts in hours.  The period itself is computed
      in the time zone that the profile names.  "effective_date" alone
      cannot show that such a period was met, so these messages MUST
      also carry in "itp_evidence" a "timestamp" stamp over the status
      message digest; a message without one is not counted.  In
      comparisons with a deadline or a window, the time of the earliest
      such stamp is used in place of "effective_at".

   due_date, remittance:  Payment details reported with an acceptance or
      a payment.  "remittance" has the form of "remittance" in the
      "payment" member (Section 6).

   assignee (object):  For "assigned": "pid", "name", and "account", a
      "payment-account" attestation (Section 7) that names the assignee.

4.  Deriving the State

   Both nodes keep every status message for an envelope whose signature
   is valid, and exchange them ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]).  The state is
   a function of that set and of the envelope, not of the order in which
   messages arrived or of the time at which a node received them.  Only
   signed content and signed evidence are used.

4.1.  Signers

   The signer roles in Section 3 are bound to the envelope core as
   follows, comparing "by" with each PID after the normalization of its
   scheme.  A participant with several PIDs signs with the PID that the
   envelope core or "assignee" names; its messages under other PIDs form
   other chains and have no role.  "Issuer" is "issuer".  "Recipient" is
   "recipient", or if it is absent, "buyer" ("seller" in self-billing).
   "Seller" and "Buyer" are "seller" and "buyer".  "Holder" is
   determined by the holder sequence in "assignee" (Section 4.3) and is
   not checked when an "assigned" message is counted: an "assigned"
   message is counted if it is validly signed by a participant or a
   bridge and continues its signer's chain, and only the messages that
   the sequence considers have effect.  "Payee" is "payment.payee.pid"
   if present, otherwise "seller", and in addition the "assignee.pid" of
   any considered "assigned" message (Section 4.3).  "Bridge" is a trust
   list entry accepted for a scheme named by a profile in "profiles", or
   for the scheme of a "tax-registration", "tax-clearance", "tax-
   report", or "receivable-registration" stamp, or of a rendition, that
   the same bridge signed for this envelope.  A bridge for the "rfc3161"
   scheme, or one that signed only "timestamp" or "network-ack" stamps,
   is not a signer of any status in this document.  "delivered" and
   "technical-rejected" are counted only if "by" is a node listed in
   "inbound" of the recipient's Participant Document.

4.2.  Counted Messages

   A message is counted if its signature is valid for its signer and
   status, its "seq" and "prev" continue the signer's chain for that
   envelope without gaps, where the chain consists of all of the
   signer's messages with a valid signature, counted or not, and it does
   not break the rules below when compared with earlier messages in the
   same signer's chain.  Two different messages with the same signer and
   "seq" stop that signer's chain at that point; both are reported.  As
   an exception, so that a signer cannot withdraw a message that others
   have relied on by signing a conflicting one, exactly one of the
   conflicting messages remains counted, and the chain continues from
   it, if it alone is named in "refers_to" or "deadline.from" by a
   message of another signer whose signature is valid and that is linked
   through "prev" digests to that signer's message with "seq" 1, or,
   failing that, if it alone carries a valid "timestamp" stamp earlier
   than any timestamp stamp of the others.  Apart from the "refers_to"
   and "deadline.from" references, rules are never evaluated against
   messages of other signers; conflicts between signers are resolved
   only by the definitions in Section 4.3.  Other messages are stored,
   returned, and reported, but not counted.  Where this document orders
   messages by "id" or takes the lowest "id", ids are compared as
   128-bit unsigned integers.

   Rules within one signer's chain:

   *  The recipient cannot send "rejected" after its own "accepted", or
      "accepted" after its own "rejected".

   *  "cancellation-refused", "payment-failed", and a "cancelled" signed
      by the recipient MUST carry "refers_to" naming a counted message
      ("cancellation-requested"; "payment-sent" or "paid"; and
      "cancellation-requested", respectively).

   *  "deemed-accepted" and "deemed-rejected" MUST carry "deadline", and
      "effective_at" when the period is in hours.  They are counted only
      if a profile in "profiles" provides that presumption (Section 5);
      "deadline.from" names a counted status message, or an accepted
      stamp, in the set; "deadline.until" is earlier than their
      "effective_date" (or "effective_at"); and "deadline.until" is not
      earlier than the start of the period plus "days" calendar days (or
      plus "hours").  The start is the "effective_date" (or
      "effective_at") of the message, or the "issued_at" of the stamp,
      named in "deadline.from".  For a period in hours, a message named
      in "deadline.from" starts it only if it carries "effective_at" and
      a "timestamp" stamp; otherwise the deemed status is not counted.
      The bound needs no knowledge of the calendar, because excluded
      days only lengthen a period.  A verifier that knows the calendar
      recomputes "until" from "deadline.from", "days" or "hours", and
      "calendar", and reports a mismatch; beyond the bound above, the
      mismatch does not affect counting.

   "received", "goods-received", and "operation-unknown" are recorded
   for information and affect no property.

4.3.  State Properties

   The state is the following set of properties.  Some belong to a
   track, and the definition of a final value names the tracks that it
   ends.

   fiscal (fiscal track):  "clearance-rejected" if a counted "clearance-
      rejected" exists; otherwise "registered" if a counted "registered"
      exists; otherwise "registration-error" if one exists; otherwise
      "pending-authority" if one exists; otherwise "none".  "clearance-
      rejected" is final for every track.

   delivery (delivery track):  "technical-rejected" if a counted
      "technical-rejected" exists; otherwise "delivered" if a counted
      "delivered" exists; otherwise "none".  "technical-rejected" is
      final for the delivery and acceptance tracks; the fiscal track
      continues.

   acceptance (acceptance track):  Let D be the earliest
      "deadline.until" among counted "deemed-accepted" messages, and R
      the earliest among counted "deemed-rejected" messages.  A counted
      "rejected" is void if D exists and the message is later than D; a
      counted "accepted" is void if R exists and the message is later
      than R.  When the deadline is a date, the message's
      "effective_date" is compared with it.  When it is a "date-time",
      the message's "effective_at" is compared as an instant, or, if the
      message has none, its "effective_date" is compared with the date
      of the deadline in the time zone that the profile names.  Where
      "effective_at" is present, "effective_date" MUST be its date in
      the time zone that the profile names.  Then: "rejected" if a
      counted "rejected" that is not void exists; otherwise "accepted"
      if a counted "accepted" that is not void exists; otherwise, if D
      and R both exist, "deemed-accepted" if D is earlier than R and
      "deemed-rejected" otherwise, and the conflict is reported;
      otherwise "deemed-accepted" if D exists; otherwise "deemed-
      rejected" if R exists; otherwise "pending".  "rejected" and
      "deemed-rejected" are final for this track.

   dispute_open:  True if the last counted message in the recipient's
      chain among "accepted", "disputed", and "rejected" is "disputed".

   assignee:  The considered "assigned" messages are, where a profile
      names an assignment registry, the counted ones signed by that
      registry's bridge, in the order of its chain; otherwise those that
      the holder sequence selects: starting with the seller as holder,
      the first counted "assigned" in the holder's chain that has not
      yet been considered is considered, and its assignee becomes the
      holder; this repeats until the holder's chain has no further
      "assigned".  A holder's later "assigned" messages are therefore
      considered only after the receivable has come back to it, and a
      second assignment by the same holder is reported and has no
      effect.  The chain of an assignee is followed only where
      reassignment is allowed (Section 5).  A message whose
      "effective_date" is earlier than that of the message that made its
      signer holder is skipped: it is never considered, and the sequence
      continues with the next counted "assigned" in the holder's chain.
      A signer is the holder when its "by" equals, after normalization,
      the seller or the "assignee.pid" concerned.  "assignee" is the
      "assignee.pid" of the last considered message whose
      "effective_date" is not later than the "effective_date" of the
      first counted "paid", in order of "effective_date" and then "id",
      at which the running sum of the "paid" amounts that "amount_paid"
      includes, less what counted "payment-failed" messages subtract
      from them, plus "amount_credited", reaches "amount_due".  If there
      is no such "paid", it is the "assignee.pid" of the last considered
      message; if there is none, none.

   amount_sent, amount_paid, amount_credited (payment track):  Sums with
      exact decimal arithmetic.  "amount_sent" is the sum of counted
      "payment-sent" amounts; "amount_paid" is the sum of counted "paid"
      amounts whose signer was payee at the message's "effective_date":
      the seller, or "payment.payee.pid" if present, before the first
      considered "assigned", and afterwards the assignee of the last
      considered "assigned" whose "effective_date" is not later;
      "amount_credited" is the sum of counted "credited" amounts.  Each
      counted "payment-failed" subtracts the smaller of its amount and
      the referenced amount, both in the invoice currency (its own
      "settled_amount" where its currency differs, and, for a failure
      without "amount" or without "settled_amount", the amount that the
      referenced message added), from "amount_sent" (if it refers to a
      "payment-sent") or "amount_paid" (if it refers to a "paid"); only
      the "payment-failed" with the lowest "id" counts for each
      referenced message.  A message without "amount" counts as the full
      "amount_due" (for payments) or "total_with_tax" (for credits).  A
      message in a currency other than the invoice currency is summed
      using its "settled_amount"; without "settled_amount" it is
      reported and not summed.

   cancellation:  "none" if "assignee" is set and a considered
      "assigned" has an "effective_date" that is not later than that of
      every counted "cancelled"; an assignment dated after a counted
      "cancelled" is reported and does not change this property.
      Otherwise "cancelled" if a counted "cancelled" exists that the
      cancellation policy allows (Section 5): under "none", none; under
      "window", one signed by the issuer or a bridge whose
      "effective_at" (or "effective_date") is within the window counted
      from the stamp that the profile names; under "request", one signed
      by the recipient that refers to a counted "cancellation-
      requested", or by a bridge; under "consent", as under "request",
      or one signed by the issuer or a bridge that carries "deadline"
      from a counted "cancellation-requested", meets the conditions on
      "deadline" in Section 4.2, and to which no counted "cancellation-
      refused" with an "effective_date" not later than "deadline.until"
      refers, or that meets an exemption the profile states as a
      condition on the envelope core; otherwise "requested" if the
      cancellation policy is "request" or "consent" and the issuer's
      last cancellation message is "cancellation-requested" and no
      counted "cancellation-refused" refers to it; otherwise "none".
      "cancelled" is final for every track except the fiscal track.

   final:  True if "fiscal" is "clearance-rejected", "delivery" is
      "technical-rejected", "acceptance" is "rejected" or "deemed-
      rejected", "cancellation" is "cancelled", or "amount_credited" is
      at least "total_with_tax".  A final value does not stop messages
      from being counted or change how other properties are computed.

   settled:  True when "amount_paid" plus "amount_credited" is at least
      "amount_due".  Being settled does not stop other messages from
      being counted; its only effect on other properties is the cut-off
      in "assignee".

   Two nodes compute the same properties when they hold the same
   envelope, the same set of messages with their evidence, and the same
   inputs that are not in that set: the accepted trust lists and
   Participant Documents, which decide whether a signer is a bridge,
   whether a key is valid, and whether a node is listed in "inbound",
   and the definitions of the profiles named in "profiles".  For
   deriving the state, the time of a "timestamp" stamp is used only if
   its time-stamping authority is accepted through a trust list, because
   an authority accepted by local configuration alone would let nodes
   compute different states.  A node that does not know a profile named
   in "profiles" reports the acceptance, cancellation, and assignee
   properties, "dispute_open", "amount_paid", and "settled" as not
   derived, and "final" as not derived unless one of its other
   conditions holds, and does not apply the defaults of Section 5.  When
   "profiles" names several profiles that set the same parameter
   differently, the first in the array that sets it applies.  Software
   that needs a single label for display can derive one, for example by
   taking the first of final, cancellation, acceptance, delivery that is
   not empty; that label is not exchanged.

   A node MAY refuse to accept a new message from a client that would
   not be counted, with "invalidTransition".  That is a local admission
   check: a message that another node has already stored is still stored
   and returned, and the definitions above decide its effect.  These
   rules are meant to be a superset of the response rules of networks
   and platforms, such as Peppol Invoice Response [PEPPOL-IR] and the
   status flows of tax authority platforms (Appendix B); a bridge to any
   of them maps only the transitions that it allows.

    fiscal:      none -> registration-error -> registered
                 none -> pending-authority  -> registered
                 none -> clearance-rejected (final, all tracks)
    delivery:    none -> delivered | technical-rejected (final)
    acceptance:  pending -> accepted | deemed-accepted | rejected
                         | deemed-rejected
                 (rejected and deemed-rejected final for this track)
    cancellation: none -> requested -> cancelled (final except fiscal)
    payments:    payment-sent -> paid, payment-failed
    assignment:  assigned (before full payment)
    corrections: credited (partial or full; full is final)

5.  Hooks for Jurisdiction Rules

   The "profiles" member of the envelope core names the profiles that
   apply.  A profile expresses the rules of a jurisdiction or network
   through the following parameters.  When "profiles" names no profile
   registered in the ITP Profiles registry, the cancellation policy is
   "none", "deemed-accepted" and "deemed-rejected" messages are not
   counted, no response window or assignment registry applies, and
   reassignment is allowed.  "none" is the default because in many tax
   regimes an issued invoice cannot be cancelled and is corrected by
   another document.

   Cancellation policy:  One of "none" (documents cannot be cancelled;
      corrections use credit notes), "window" (the issuer can cancel
      within a period given in the profile, which can be counted in
      hours from the registration or validation time in an accepted
      stamp), "request" (the issuer requests and the recipient answers),
      or "consent" (as "request", but some cases need no answer, as the
      profile lists).

   Time zone:  The time zone name from the IANA Time Zone Database in
      which dates are compared and days are counted.  The default is
      UTC.

   Deadlines:  For each status that the law requires within a period,
      the event the period starts from, its length, and its calendar.
      Calendars are registered in the ITP Calendars registry.  The
      pattern "business-days-XX", where XX is an [ISO3166] alpha-2 code
      or an [ISO3166-2] subdivision code (for example "business-days-MY-
      01"), names a calendar whose definition states the weekly rest
      days and the public holidays it excludes.  Weekly rest days are
      not Saturday and Sunday everywhere, and holidays can differ
      between the regions of one country.  Days are counted in the time
      zone that the profile names.  Unless the profile says otherwise,
      the first day counted is the day after the event, "until" is the
      last day of the period, and a period in hours is elapsed time to
      which no calendar applies.  Holidays that are fixed by
      announcement shortly before they occur, as lunar holidays often
      are, are applied as announced; a verifier that recomputes "until"
      reports a mismatch caused by a later announcement without treating
      it as an error.  A verifier that does not know a calendar does not
      recompute deadlines that use it.

   Deemed acceptance or rejection:  Whether acceptance, or in some
      regimes rejection, is presumed after a deadline, and its legal
      effect, for example that the invoice becomes enforceable as a
      negotiable instrument.

   Response windows:  For each recipient status, such as "rejected", the
      period after which the recipient can no longer send it, with no
      presumption attached.  The window starts from an event that the
      profile names and that is in the set, such as the "effective_date"
      of "delivered" or the "issued_at" of an accepted stamp, and is
      counted in calendar days or in hours, so that every node can
      compute its end.  A message whose "effective_date" (or
      "effective_at") is after the end of its window is not counted.  A
      window that the law counts in business days is expressed through a
      deemed status with "deadline", or is only reported, and does not
      affect counting.  Declining a rejection, which some regimes allow
      the issuer, has no ITP status in this version.

   Preconditions:  Whether delivery requires a prior "registered"
      status, as in clearance regimes.

   Numbering:  The scope in which "invoice_number" is unique, for
      duplicate detection, for example per tax identifier and fiscal
      year, per establishment, or per document type and point of sale.

   Assignment registries:  Where assignments of receivables are recorded
      by an official registry, the bridge records the assignment and
      adds a "receivable-registration" stamp to the envelope.

   Reassignment:  Whether an assignee can assign the receivable again.
      Where no registered profile in "profiles" says otherwise,
      reassignment is allowed.

6.  Payment Information

   The optional "payment" member of the envelope core
   [I-D.martinez-itp-core] contains:

   payee (optional, object):  "pid" and "name" of the payee when it is
      not the seller, for example a factor named in the invoice.

   due_date (optional, string):  The payment due date.

   schedule (optional, array):  Installments, each with "due_date" and
      "amount".

   prepaid_amount (optional, string):  The amount paid in advance, for
      example as a prepayment.

   methods (optional, array):  Objects with "type" from the ITP Payment
      Method Types registry.  The initial type is "credit-transfer",
      whose object contains "account", a "payment-account" attestation
      (Section 7) that names the payee, and optionally "rails", values
      from the ITP Payment Rails registry over which the payee accepts
      payment to that account (for example "sepa-sct", "ach", "fednow",
      "spei", "pix", "upi", "paynow", or "mpesa").  The account scheme
      says how the account is identified; the rail says how the money
      moves, and one account is often reachable over several rails.  A
      payer that does not support a method ignores it.

      Accounts addressed through an alias that a central directory
      resolves at payment time, such as a PIX key, a UPI address, or a
      mobile number on a proxy-addressed instant payment system, can be
      re-pointed by their holder.  An attestation of such an alias shows
      the binding at the time it was issued; payers resolve the alias
      again and compare the holder before paying.  For mobile money, the
      scheme is "mobile-money", and its value names the provider and
      country as well as the identifier (an E.164 number, or a merchant
      short code with an account number).  "msisdn" is used for a mobile
      number registered as an alias on a proxy-addressed instant payment
      system; other aliases of such systems, such as a business
      registration number, are registered as their own schemes.

   remittance (optional, object):  "creditor_reference", a creditor
      reference in a format the rail defines, such as [ISO11649] (at
      most 25 characters), a Swiss QR reference, or a Norwegian KID
      number, and "unstructured", free text to be placed in the
      remittance information of the payment, for example in an
      [ISO20022] message.  Length limits are those of the rail and
      differ widely: 140 characters in SEPA, 210 in Russian payment
      orders, 12 in some mobile money references.  A "credit-transfer"
      method MAY carry its own "remittance", which applies to payments
      over its rails in place of this one.  If neither is given, payers
      use the envelope id where the rail allows 36 characters, and
      otherwise "invoice_number" cut to the rail's limit.  A payment
      that settles several invoices carries a reference per invoice
      where the rail allows it, or a separate remittance advice.

   A relying party MUST NOT treat payment details as verified while the
   key that signed the envelope, or the key that signed an "assigned"
   message, is "recently bound" ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]).  A receiving
   node that interprets the document, where "methods" is present, and
   finds in the document a payment account that is not equal to an
   account attested in "methods" rejects the envelope with
   "inconsistentCore".

   An "assigned" status MUST be signed, with "itp_role" "issuer" or
   "delegated-issuer" and not with a node key, by the current holder of
   the receivable: the seller or, where the applicable law lets a
   receivable be assigned again, the assignee of the last considered
   "assigned" message.  Where a profile names an assignment registry,
   the registry's bridge signs "assigned" with "itp_role" "bridge" once
   the registry records the assignment.  Every "assigned" status MUST
   carry in "assignee" a "payment-account" attestation that names the
   assignee.  Assignment can be subject to the law of the place of the
   assignor; the United Nations Convention on the Assignment of
   Receivables in International Trade [UN-ASSIGN] is one framework.
   "assigned" records only who is entitled to payment.  It does not
   imply a sale or a discount.

7.  Payment Account Attestations

   This document registers the attestation type "payment-account" in the
   ITP Attestation Types registry.  It binds an account to its holder.
   Its claims are:

   account:  An object with "scheme", from the ITP Account Schemes
      registry, and "value", the account identifier in the format of
      that scheme, for example an [ISO13616] IBAN for "iban", which is
      used inside and outside SEPA, or a sort code and account number
      for "gb-sortcode".

   holder_name, lang:  The holder's name as the account-servicing
      institution records it, as a string or as an array of objects with
      "lang" and "text" when the institution records it in more than one
      script.  Institutions can record names in a reduced character set,
      such as half-width katakana in Japan, or transliterated, so
      software compares the holder name with the payee's name only
      through the payee verification service of the rail, not by string
      comparison.

   checked_by (optional):  The name of the payee verification service
      that was queried, if the attestation issuer obtained the result
      from one (see Section 8).

   The holder is the subject of the attestation, given in "sub".  It is
   issued by the institution that services the account, or by a service
   that queries that institution, and its validity period is at most 90
   days.  When the attestation is carried in "payment.methods" or in
   "assignee", the disclosure of "account" MUST be included, and "sub"
   MUST be the URN of "payment.payee.pid" (or, if "payment.payee" is
   absent, of the seller) or of "assignee.pid", respectively.  A
   "payment-account" attestation MUST NOT be listed in "attestations" of
   a Participant Document, which anyone can retrieve.

8.  Application Considerations for Payment

   This section is advice to software that pays invoices on the basis of
   ITP data; it does not constrain the protocol.  Such software is
   expected:

   *  not to pay automatically to an account that is not covered by a
      valid "payment-account" attestation naming the payee, and to check
      the status of that attestation when it initiates the payment;

   *  where the payer's institution or the payment system offers
      verification of the payee, for example Verification of Payee in
      the European Union ([EU-IPR], [EPC-VOP]), Confirmation of Payee
      for "gb-sortcode" accounts, or the name that proxy-addressed and
      mobile money systems show before the payer confirms, not to
      proceed automatically on a result other than a full match, and not
      to rely on an opt-out from that verification for the first payment
      to an account; and

   *  to ask a person to approve the first payment to an account not
      used before for the same payee, and the first payment after an
      "assigned" status.

   Payment details that are not verified because a key is recently bound
   are not used automatically.  Nodes that also initiate payments or
   access payment accounts do so under the rules that apply to those
   activities.

10.  Security Considerations

   The security considerations of [I-D.martinez-itp-core] apply.

   Statuses can have legal and tax effects: an acceptance can make an
   invoice enforceable, and some jurisdictions report acceptance,
   rejection, and payment to the tax authority.  This document therefore
   binds each status to the party entitled to sign it.  Nodes can sign
   commercial statuses only with "delegated-issuer" keys that the
   participant has listed.  The state is derived from the set of
   messages, so an attacker that delays or reorders messages cannot make
   the two parties see different states once they have exchanged the
   same messages, although it can delay them.

   Fraud based on notifying changed bank details is addressed by binding
   accounts to attestations from the institutions that service them, by
   payee verification at payment time, by human approval of new accounts
   and of the first payment after an assignment, and by the 72-hour rule
   of the core document.  A forged "assigned" status redirects payment
   in the same way, so it needs the holder's own key and an attestation
   for the assignee's account.  These controls do not stop a seller
   whose own systems are compromised.

   Deemed acceptance depends on dates.  The "effective_date" of a
   rejection is chosen by its signer, so a recipient could back-date it,
   and the "deadline.until" of a deemed acceptance is chosen by its
   signer too.  Verifiers recompute deadlines when they know the
   calendar, and profiles where timing has legal effect can require a
   timestamp stamp in the "itp_evidence" of status messages
   ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]).  Local receipt times are never used,
   because the two nodes would compute different states.

11.  Privacy Considerations

   Status messages reveal the commercial relationship and payment
   behavior of the parties, and payment-account attestations reveal
   account details.  Status messages are exchanged only between the
   nodes of the parties, are not registered in transparency services,
   and are stored under the same protections as envelopes
   ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]).  Payment-account attestations are
   registered as the core document requires for all attestations.  The
   log does not learn the account, but for a legal entity the PID digest
   can be reversed by testing identifiers and the Signed Statement
   identifies the attestation issuer, so a reader of the log can learn
   that a given institution attested an account of that legal entity,
   and when.

   Account identifiers such as mobile numbers, UPI addresses, and PIX
   keys that are telephone numbers, e-mail addresses, or tax identifiers
   are personal data when the holder is a natural person, and
   "holder_name" then names that person.  "account" and "holder_name"
   are selectively disclosable, and the payee discloses them only in
   envelopes and status messages addressed to the payer.

12.  Internationalization Considerations

   The considerations of [I-D.martinez-itp-core] apply.  "reason.text",
   "conditions", and "holder_name" are human-readable and carry "lang".
   Business-day calendars depend on weekly rest days and on national and
   regional holidays, some fixed by lunar calendars, and are defined by
   profiles, not by this document.

Appendix D.  State Derivation Examples

   Implementations are expected to compute the following properties.
   "R" is the recipient, "I" the issuer, and "B" a bridge.  Each set of
   messages gives the same result in any order of arrival.  In examples
   2 and 7, the set also contains a counted "delivered" that
   "deadline.from" names, and the deadline meets the conditions in
   Section 4.2.

   1.  R: "accepted" (seq 1), then R: "disputed" (seq 2).  acceptance is
       "accepted" and dispute_open is true.

   2.  Under a profile with deemed acceptance, I: "deemed-accepted" with
       "deadline.until" 2026-11-03 and "effective_date" 2026-11-04; R:
       "rejected" with "effective_date" 2026-11-05.  The rejection is
       void; acceptance is "deemed-accepted".  With a rejection dated
       2026-11-02 instead, the rejection counts and acceptance is
       "rejected".

   3.  R (the buyer): "payment-sent" for 100.00 and for 200.00 on an
       invoice with "amount_due" 300.00; then a "payment-failed" signed
       by the buyer that refers to the first.  amount_sent is 200.00.

   4.  Two different messages from R with "seq" 2, neither referenced by
       another signer's message and neither carrying a "timestamp"
       stamp.  R's chain stops at seq 1; both messages are reported and
       neither counts.

   5.  B: "registered"; then R: "rejected".  fiscal is "registered" and
       acceptance is "rejected", so final is already true; an I:
       "credited" for the total still counts, and amount_credited equals
       "total_with_tax".

   6.  Under a profile with the cancellation policy "request", I (the
       seller): "assigned" to a factor; then I: "cancellation-
       requested". cancellation stays "none" instead of "requested",
       because the receivable was assigned.

   7.  Under a profile with deemed rejection, I: "deemed-rejected" with
       "deadline.until" 2026-11-10 and "effective_date" 2026-11-11; R:
       "accepted" with "effective_date" 2026-11-12.  The acceptance is
       void, and acceptance is "deemed-rejected".

   8.  I (the seller): "assigned" to factor F1 (seq 1); F1: "assigned"
       to F2 (seq 1 in F1's chain), under a profile that allows
       reassignment and names no registry.  assignee is F2.

Appendix E.  Open Issues

   1.  Whether the lifecycle statuses, which mirror business processes
       defined by networks such as Peppol and by tax authorities, belong
       in an IETF document, or should be a vocabulary maintained
       elsewhere that ITP messages refer to.

   2.  Initial entries of the ITP Calendars, Account Schemes, and
       Payment Rails registries.

   3.  Alignment of reason codes with Peppol and national codes.

   4.  Whether status messages whose timing has legal effect should
       always carry a timestamp stamp.
