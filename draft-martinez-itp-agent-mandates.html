Invoice Trust Protocol (ITP): Agent Mandates

Abstract

This document lets a business authorize a software agent to issue, accept, reject, or dispute invoices, and to report payments, through the Invoice Trust Protocol (ITP), within limits that the business sets. An authorization is a mandate: a Selective Disclosure JSON Web Token (SD-JWT) credential issued by the business to a key held by the agent and enforced by a named ITP node that records each use.¶

1. Introduction

Some purchasing and accounts-payable software lets automated agents send invoices and status messages. A counterparty that receives one needs to know which business the agent acts for and whether the action is within the agent's authority. The business that deployed the agent needs its limits enforced even when the agent misbehaves, for example because text in an invoice it processed changed its behavior.¶

The core of the Invoice Trust Protocol (ITP) [I-D.martinez-itp-core] defines the "agent" signer role and the "agent" member of the envelope core but leaves their content to this document. This document defines:¶

  • the mandate, an SD-JWT VC [I-D.ietf-oauth-sd-jwt-vc] that binds an agent key to a participant and to a set of permitted actions and limits (Section 3);¶

  • how an agent signs envelopes and status messages and proves possession of its key for each one (Section 4);¶

  • enforcement of limits by a single node, which prevents the same budget from being spent through several nodes (Section 5);¶

  • attenuated delegation from one agent to another (Section 6); and¶

  • approval by a person above a threshold (Section 7).¶

Payment through HTTP-based payment protocols, which raises anti-money-laundering, sanctions-screening, and payee-verification questions, is out of scope.¶

OAuth 2.0 Rich Authorization Requests [RFC9396] can express limits such as amounts, and OAuth 2.0 Token Exchange [RFC8693] can record a chain of delegation. Both describe what an authorization server grants to a client for access to a resource. A mandate is instead issued by a participant with its own keys, presented to counterparties that do not share an authorization server with it, and enforced by a node that records each use. The Agent Payments Protocol [AP2], now being standardized in the FIDO Alliance, also uses mandates, as verifiable digital credentials, for payments by agents; ITP mandates cover invoices and their statuses and do not give access to payment instruments. The two are complementary: between businesses, an invoice usually comes before its payment, so an agent can accept an invoice under an ITP mandate and then pay it under an AP2 mandate, and report the payment with the "pay" action. Aligning the two is left for future work. Frameworks for agent identity such as [I-D.ietf-wimse-aims] identify an agent as a workload and authenticate it to services; how an agent authenticates to its enforcing node is outside this document, and the key bound by a mandate can be one that such a framework manages. A mandate states which business the agent acts for and within which limits, which a workload identity does not.¶

1.1. Overview

This section is informative. It summarizes the parts of [I-D.martinez-itp-core] and [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle] that this document relies on, and how a mandate is used.¶

In ITP, an invoice travels inside an envelope: a JWS whose payload, the envelope core, carries the invoice and data such as its issuer and recipient, and whose signatures each state a signer role ("itp_role"). A change in the state of an invoice after delivery, such as acceptance, rejection, or a reported payment, is a status message: a signed object that refers to the envelope by its identifier and digest, and that each signer chains to its previous ones. Each participant publishes a Participant Document with its keys, the nodes that send for it ("senders"), and the keys that those nodes hold for it ("delegates"). Keys that are not listed in "delegates" stay with the participant.¶

A mandate is used as follows:¶

  1. The principal signs a mandate for a key that the agent holds and gives it to the agent. It gives the enforcing node the mandate with all its disclosures.¶

  2. To act, the agent builds the envelope core or status message that it wants to send, places a presentation of the mandate in its "agent" member, and signs the payload with its key (Section 4). The presentation is bound to that payload and cannot be moved to another document.¶

  3. The agent sends the signed payload to the enforcing node. The node checks the mandate and the limits, reserves the amount, asks a person to approve if needed, records the use, and adds two signatures: a "delegated-issuer" signature, so that the document carries an issuer-level signature, and an "operator-seal" signature, which states that the use was checked (Section 5).¶

  4. The enforcing node sends the document to the counterparty, which checks the signatures and the disclosed parts of the mandate and relies on the seal for the limits (Section 5.1).¶

1.2. The Experiment

This document is Experimental. The experiment is whether at least two independent implementations of ITP nodes enforce mandates issued by each other's participants, using the mandate in Appendix B, and whether the limits defined here are enough for the purchasing and accounts-payable uses that implementers report.¶

Results will be reported on the mailing list where this document is discussed and in later revisions of it. The experiment ends 24 months after publication, together with that of [I-D.martinez-itp-core].¶

2. Conventions and Definitions

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.¶

Terms defined in [I-D.martinez-itp-core] and [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle] are used with the same meaning. In addition:¶

Principal:

The participant that issues a mandate.¶

Agent:

Software that holds the private key bound by a mandate and acts for the principal.¶

Enforcing node:

The ITP node named in the mandate that checks and records every use of it.¶

3. Mandates

A mandate is an SD-JWT VC [I-D.ietf-oauth-sd-jwt-vc], with "typ" "dc+sd-jwt", whose issuer-signed JWT is signed with a key from the principal's accepted Participant Document ([I-D.martinez-itp-core], "Trust and Keys") that is not listed in "delegates". An operator that holds only delegated keys therefore cannot authorize an agent on its own. This is the issuer key discovery mechanism for mandates in the sense of Section 2.5 of [I-D.ietf-oauth-sd-jwt-vc]; JWT VC Issuer Metadata is not used. The claims are:¶

iss (required):

The principal's PID in URN form ("urn:ietf:params:itp:pid:...").¶

sub (required):

An identifier of the agent chosen by the principal, unique among its mandates. It SHOULD NOT identify a natural person.¶

vct (required):

"urn:ietf:params:itp:mandate".¶

cnf (required):

The agent's public key, as a "jwk" confirmation method [RFC7800]. The JWK contains "alg".¶

aud (required):

The Node Document URL of the enforcing node. Counterparties and other relying parties process the mandate without being named in "aud"; for mandates, this overrides the rejection rule of Section 4.1.3 of [RFC7519]. That node MUST be listed in "senders" of the principal's Participant Document, MUST have a key listed in "delegates" of the principal's Participant Document with that node as "node", and MUST be the node through which the principal signs status messages, so that the principal's chain of status messages does not fork.¶

iat, nbf, exp (required):

As in [RFC7519]. Mandates SHOULD NOT be valid for more than 30 days.¶

status (required):

A reference to a status list entry [I-D.ietf-oauth-status-list], so that the principal can revoke the mandate at once. The Status List Token is a JWT signed with a key of the principal that is not listed in "delegates", or with a key listed in "kids" of the principal's "senders" entry for the enforcing node. For a delegated mandate, the enforcing node provides the status list entry.¶

actions (required):

An array of values from the ITP Mandate Actions registry (Section 12.5); the initial values are in Table 1.¶

limits (required):

An object with "currency" and any of: "per_document" (maximum amount of one document or payment), "per_period" (maximum total within "period", which is then required), "period" (a duration of whole days or weeks in the "duration" grammar of Appendix A of [RFC3339], for example "P30D" or "P1W"), "counterparties" (an array of PIDs the agent may deal with; a counterparty matches when a PID verified for its accepted Participant Document equals an entry), "document_types" (values of the ITP Document Types registry), and "approval_above" (an amount above which every action needs approval by a person, Section 7). Amounts are "amount" values as defined in [I-D.martinez-itp-core]. Limits are compared with, and reservations made for, the absolute value of the amount of an action, so that a negative total, as in some credit notes, never increases the remaining budget.¶

max_delegation (optional):

The number of further delegations allowed (Section 6). The default is 0.¶

software (optional):

An object that describes the software in which the agent runs, as stated by the principal: "software_id" and "software_version", with the meanings given to these names in Section 2 of [RFC7591], and "provider", the name of the organization that supplies the software. It lets the principal and the enforcing node find and revoke every mandate held by a given product or version, for example after a vulnerability in it becomes known. It is not evidence that the agent runs that software: relying parties MUST NOT base trust decisions on it. The model or other components that the software uses change more often than mandates and are not described here; a deployment that needs them records them in its own logs.¶

Claims not registered in the "JSON Web Token Claims" registry, such as "actions", "limits", "max_delegation", "software", and "principal", are private claim names (Section 4.3 of [RFC7519]) whose meaning is given by this document for the "vct" values "urn:ietf:params:itp:mandate" and "urn:ietf:params:itp:mandate-delegated" only.¶

Table 1: Initial mandate actions
Action Permits
issue Issuing envelopes of the document types in "limits"
accept Status "accepted"
reject Status "rejected"
dispute Status "disputed"
pay Status "payment-sent"

None of these actions gives access to a payment account. The "pay" action lets an agent report a payment it has made by other means, and reserves the amount against its limits.¶

Claims other than "iss", "vct", "cnf", "aud", the time claims, and "status" MAY be selectively disclosable, so that a principal can avoid showing all its limits to every counterparty. A counterparty sees the disclosures that the agent presents and relies on the enforcing node for the rest. Because the presentation in the "agent" member (Section 4) is the one that counterparties receive, the principal, or for a delegated mandate the agent that issued it, gives the enforcing node the mandate with all its disclosures, by means outside this document.¶

4. Using a Mandate

An agent signs an envelope or status message with a signature whose protected header has "itp_role" "agent", "crit" including "itp_role", "alg", "typ", and "kid" set to the JWK SHA-256 Thumbprint [RFC7638] of the key in "cnf". The payload then contains:¶

{
  "agent": {
    "mandate": "<SD-JWT with selected disclosures and KB-JWT>",
    "enforced_by": "https://node.example.com/itp",
    "use": "01a18210-0000-7000-8000-000000000001"
  }
}
¶

For a delegated mandate, "mandate" is a Delegate SD-JWT presentation of the whole chain (Section 6), whose final Key Binding JWT is the one described below.¶

Replay protection comes from the Key Binding JWT ([RFC9901]) of the presented SD-JWT, which has "aud" set to the enforcing node and "nonce" set to a value that the enforcing node issued to the agent. The enforcing node accepts each nonce once, within a validity period that it chooses and that SHOULD NOT exceed five minutes, and refuses with "mandateInvalid" a presentation whose nonce is unknown, expired, or already used. How the agent obtains the nonce, like how it authenticates to the node, is outside this document. "use" is a UUIDv7 chosen by the agent that identifies this use in the record that the enforcing node keeps (Section 5); the node refuses a "use" value that it has already recorded. The Key Binding JWT otherwise follows Section 4.3 of [RFC9901]. Its "sd_hash" covers the presented SD-JWT. The agent signature, made with the same key, covers the payload that contains the presentation, and so binds the presentation to the action.¶

Status messages signed by an agent use the General JSON Serialization, so that they can carry the agent signature and the signatures added by the enforcing node.¶

The agent sets every member of the envelope core, including "id", "issuer_node", and "transparency_salt", or of the status message, including "seq" and "prev", before it signs. The enforcing node, as the principal's issuing node, refuses with "mandateInvalid" a payload in which they do not meet [I-D.martinez-itp-core], for example a status message that does not continue the principal's chain.¶

An "agent" signature is never accepted in place of an issuer signature ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]). For issuance, the enforcing node adds a "delegated-issuer" signature after it has checked the mandate, so the envelope still has an issuer-level signature, and an "operator-seal" signature that records the use. For status messages it adds the same two signatures.¶

5. Enforcement

Only the enforcing node named in "aud" can authorize a use of a mandate. Before it signs or forwards anything for the agent, it MUST:¶

  1. verify the mandate signature against a key of the principal's accepted Participant Document that is not listed in "delegates" and, for a delegated mandate, verify the chain as specified in [I-D.ietf-oauth-delegate-sd-jwt], each delegation being signed with the "cnf" key of the mandate before it, and check the validity period and status of each mandate in the chain;¶

  2. verify the Key Binding JWT and its nonce, and the agent signature, and check "use";¶

  3. check that the principal (the "iss" of the root mandate) is the "issuer" of the envelope or the "by" of the status message, that the principal has the signer role that [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle] gives for the status, that the action is in "actions", and that the document and counterparty meet "limits", for every mandate in the chain; the counterparty is the "recipient" for "issue" and, for status messages, whichever of "seller" and "buyer" is not the principal;¶

  4. refuse the action with "mandateInvalid" if the currency of its amount (the envelope "currency", or the "currency" of the status message, which defaults to it) differs from "limits.currency", and otherwise atomically reserve the amount against "per_period" of every mandate in the chain; a principal that acts in several currencies issues one mandate per currency; and¶

  5. if the amount exceeds "approval_above", hold the action until an approval is received (Section 7), answering with status 202 as for asynchronous results in [I-D.martinez-itp-core].¶

The enforcing node also holds for approval an "accept" or "pay" action on an envelope signed with a recently bound key ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]), or from an issuer that is not in "limits.counterparties" and has not issued an envelope to the principal before, whatever the amount.¶

The enforcing node keeps a tamper-evident record of each use, with its "use" identifier, the "sub" and "cnf" key thumbprint of each mandate in the chain, the principal, the action, the digest of the envelope or status message, the decision, its reason and time, any approval, and the "software" claim if present, for at least as long as the principal retains the documents concerned. This is the record that lets the principal and auditors establish which agent did what, in line with the audit records that agent identity frameworks such as [I-D.ietf-wimse-aims] require. A request whose envelope core or status message payload, with the "agent" member removed, equals that of a recorded use is a retry, which the agent sends with a new "use" value and nonce: the node returns the result of the recorded use and reserves nothing more. A request with the envelope or status message "id" of a recorded use and a payload that, with the "agent" member removed, differs from that of the recorded use is refused with "mandateInvalid". The enforcing node's "operator-seal" signature covers the payload, and so the "agent" member and the "use" identifier.¶

Reservations are kept per mandate, envelope, and use. "issue" reserves the "total_with_tax" of the envelope and is released when the state of the envelope ([I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle]) shows delivery "technical-rejected", fiscal "clearance-rejected", cancellation "cancelled", or acceptance "rejected" or "deemed-rejected". "accept" reserves the "amount" of the "accepted" message or, if it has none, the "total_with_tax" of the envelope, and is released when acceptance becomes "rejected" or "deemed-rejected", which includes the case in which the "accepted" message is void ([I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle]). "pay" reserves the amount of the "payment-sent" message and is released only by a "payment-failed" that refers to it and is signed by the principal or by a bridge; a "payment-failed" signed by the payee does not release it. "reject" and "dispute" reserve nothing. A reservation for an action that is held for approval is released when the approval is refused, or when the principal or the agent withdraws the action before it is approved. A "pay" reservation for an envelope on which the same mandate holds an "accept" reservation reduces that "accept" reservation by the amount paid, so that accepting and paying one invoice is counted once. A reservation is never released because time has passed, since a payment may already have been made. "per_period" is a window that ends at the time of the reservation, and a reservation counts toward the period in which the use occurred. A reservation is held while none of its release conditions holds in the current state, and is held again if the state changes so that none holds.¶

Because each mandate has a single enforcing node, the same budget cannot be spent twice by sending through two nodes. A principal that wants an agent to work through several nodes issues one mandate per node, each with its own limits.¶

5.1. Processing by Counterparties

A counterparty does not evaluate the limits of a mandate, and usually cannot, since the principal can keep them undisclosed (Section 3). It checks who acted and that the enforcing node vouches for the use. A counterparty node MUST reject with "mandateInvalid" an envelope or status message with an "agent" member unless:¶

  1. it carries an "operator-seal" signature made with a key listed in "kids" of the principal's "senders" entry for "enforced_by", and that node is the "aud" of the mandate;¶

  2. the agent signature verifies with the key in "cnf" of the mandate presented in "mandate";¶

  3. the root mandate verifies with a key of the principal's accepted Participant Document that is not listed in "delegates", the chain of a delegated mandate verifies as specified in [I-D.ietf-oauth-delegate-sd-jwt] (Section 6), and every mandate in the chain was valid at the signing time;¶

  4. the root mandate's "iss" (or "principal") is the URN of the envelope's "issuer" or of the status message's "by"; and¶

  5. the action is among the disclosed "actions".¶

The other signatures are checked as for any document sent through a node ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]). When all checks pass, the counterparty treats the document as sent by the principal, and the "operator-seal" as the enforcing node's statement that the use was within the limits of every mandate in the chain and was approved where Section 7 requires it. A counterparty that does not trust a given enforcing node can refuse documents signed by agents through it.¶

This is why "aud" names the enforcing node even though counterparties also process the mandate: "aud" says which node may authorize uses, and so whose seal a counterparty accepts. A counterparty is never the audience of a mandate and does not reject it for that reason.¶

6. Delegation

An agent whose mandate has "max_delegation" greater than 0 can pass part of its authority to another agent. Delegation uses Delegate SD-JWT [I-D.ietf-oauth-delegate-sd-jwt], in which the Key Binding JWT of one SD-JWT becomes itself an SD-JWT that carries the next credential.¶

The delegating agent creates a Key Binding SD-JWT with "typ" "kb+sd-jwt+kb", signed with the key in "cnf" of its own mandate. Its Delegate Payload is the delegated mandate: it has "vct" "urn:ietf:params:itp:mandate-delegated", a "principal" claim with the principal's PID in URN form, "cnf" with the key of the new agent, the same "aud" as the mandate it extends, "actions" and "limits" that are equal to or narrower than those of that mandate, an "exp" no later than its "exp", "max_delegation" lower than its "max_delegation", and the other claims of Section 3 except "iss". A limit that is absent from the delegated mandate is inherited. The Key Binding SD-JWT uses "issuer_jwt_hash" and not "sd_hash", so that the new agent can leave out disclosures of earlier mandates when it presents the chain. Further delegations append further Key Binding SD-JWTs.¶

The agent presents the chain, ending with a Key Binding JWT as in Section 4, in the "mandate" member. Each mandate in it carries only the disclosures that the agent chooses, so counterparties see only what is disclosed to them. The enforcing node receives every mandate in the chain with all its disclosures, from the principal for the root and from the delegating agent for the others, by means outside this document, so that it can check that the limits are narrower. It checks the whole chain, counts every use against every mandate in it, and SHOULD refuse chains longer than five mandates.¶

7. Approval by a Person

When "approval_above" is exceeded, or when the principal's policy at the enforcing node requires it, the node asks the principal for approval. An approval is a JWS in the Flattened JSON Serialization with "typ" "itp-approval+jws", signed with a key of a natural person whom the principal has authorized and registered with the enforcing node; how that registration is done is local configuration. That key SHOULD be held only by that person, for example as a non-exportable key in a browser or an authenticator: an approval signed with a key that the enforcing node holds does not show that the person approved. Its payload contains "mandate_sub", "action", "envelope_digest" (or the status message digest), "amount", "currency", and "iat". The principal can instead approve through a verifiable presentation, for example with [OPENID4VP], whose transaction data (Appendix B.3.3 of [OPENID4VP]) carries the same members, so that the presentation is bound to the action. The enforcing node keeps the approval with the record of the use. ITP status messages are never treated as approvals. The enforcing node shows the approver the action as stated in the signed envelope core or status message, and the document, and not a description written by the agent, since an agent manipulated by content it processed can describe an action misleadingly.¶

Other mechanisms in which an authorization waits for a person, such as the deferred token responses under discussion in the OAuth working group, could carry the approval instead. Whether to use one is left for a later revision.¶

9. Security Considerations

The security considerations of [I-D.martinez-itp-core] and [I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle] apply.¶

A mandate limits what a compromised agent can do. Text in invoices, reason fields, and attachments is written by counterparties and can try to change an agent's behavior. Because enforcement happens at the node, outside the agent, such text cannot raise the agent's limits; a manipulated agent can still act within its limits. For example, it can split an amount into several documents or payments below "per_document" and "approval_above", which only "per_period" bounds, and it can reject or dispute any envelope, since these actions reserve nothing. Principals that need tighter control use "counterparties", short validity periods, or a lower "approval_above", and enforcing nodes MAY limit the rate of actions per mandate.¶

Mandates are signed only with keys that the principal holds, so an operator cannot issue mandates to itself. The enforcing node is nevertheless trusted with the limits: a compromised enforcing node can approve any action that the principal's "delegated-issuer" key can sign. Principals review the uses it records and monitor the transparency logs where its envelopes are registered.¶

Revocation takes effect when relying parties refresh the status list. Enforcing nodes SHOULD check the status of every mandate in the chain at each use, using a Status List Token no older than five minutes, and an enforcing node that provides the status list applies a revocation as soon as it records it; counterparties rely on the enforcing node for timely revocation.¶

The Key Binding JWT names the enforcing node and contains a nonce that the node issued and accepts only once, and the agent signature covers the payload that contains it, so neither the presentation nor the signed payload can be replayed, whatever the clocks of the agent and the node. The reservation is atomic, so concurrent requests cannot exceed "per_period", and it is released only by a signed status, so the amount of a payment already made cannot be reused.¶

The agent's private key SHOULD be generated and kept in the environment where the agent runs. If a node or another intermediary signs for the agent, the key in "cnf" no longer identifies the agent, and the mandate limits only what that intermediary does.¶

Selective disclosure hides limits from counterparties, so a counterparty cannot check them itself and depends on the "operator-seal" of the enforcing node. Counterparties that do not trust that node can refuse envelopes signed by agents.¶

10. Privacy Considerations

A mandate reveals the principal's PID and, through disclosed claims, part of its purchasing policy. A disclosed "software" claim reveals which product the principal uses; principals can keep it undisclosed to counterparties, since only the enforcing node needs it. The enforcing node learns every action of the agent, which it needs to enforce limits. Counterparties that receive many documents from the same principal, or that share what they receive, could assemble its purchasing policy from the disclosures they see. To limit this, principals SHOULD keep "limits" and "software" undisclosed to counterparties, agents SHOULD disclose of "actions" only what the action needs, and principals SHOULD add decoy digests ([RFC9901]) so that the number of undisclosed claims does not reveal the policy. In a delegated mandate, an agent discloses of each mandate in the chain only what it chooses (Section 6). Approvals identify the person who approved, and are kept only by the enforcing node and the principal.¶

11. Internationalization Considerations

Mandates and approvals contain no human-readable text. Amounts and currencies follow [I-D.martinez-itp-core].¶

Appendix A. Worked Example

This appendix is informative. It follows one use of the mandate in Appendix B.¶

  1. A business registered for VAT in the United Kingdom deploys an accounts-payable agent. In its node's administration interface it creates the agent's key pair, or registers a key that the agent software generated, and signs a mandate for it with a key of its Participant Document that is not listed in "delegates". The mandate allows "accept", "reject", and "dispute", up to 5000.00 GBP per document and 40000.00 GBP in 30 days, for one supplier, with approval by a person above 2500.00 GBP. The business's node is the enforcing node.¶

  2. The supplier delivers an envelope with an invoice of 3000.00 GBP to that node ([I-D.martinez-itp-core]).¶

  3. The agent reads the invoice and decides to accept it. It obtains a nonce from the enforcing node, creates the "accepted" status message with the "agent" member (Section 4), and signs it with its key.¶

  4. The enforcing node runs the checks in Section 5. The mandate is valid, the action is allowed, the supplier is in "counterparties", and 3000.00 GBP fits both limits, so the node reserves the amount. Because it exceeds "approval_above", the node answers with status 202 and asks an authorized person to approve, showing the signed status message and the invoice.¶

  5. After the approval (Section 7), the node adds its "delegated-issuer" and "operator-seal" signatures, records the use, and sends the status message to the supplier's node.¶

  6. The supplier's node checks the "agent" member as described in Section 5.1 and counts the status message as an acceptance by the business ([I-D.martinez-itp-lifecycle]).¶

Had the invoice contained text that led the agent to accept 50000.00 GBP, step 4 would have refused the action, because the limits are checked by the node and not by the agent.¶
